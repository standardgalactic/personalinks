Temporal Diversification of Engagement


The expected marginal compression gain formalism from \emph{The Geometry of Boredom}
extends naturally from one task to a portfolio of unresolved scopes operating at unequal
horizons. Let
\[
\mathcal{T}=\{(S_i,\tau_i)\}_{i=1}^n,\qquad S_i=(O_i,\tau_i),\qquad \tau_1\ll\tau_2\ll\cdots\ll\tau_n,
\]
where each scope $S_i$ has a local option space $O_i$ and characteristic resolution horizon
$\tau_i$.

\paragraph{Scope note.}
This section is a formal extension proposal and should not be read as an implementation claim
about the repository's pending Appendix B poset semantics.

At time $t$, with accumulated history $H_t$ and current operator $F_t=C(H_t)$, define local
expected marginal compression gain
\[
\mathrm{EMCG}_i(a\mid F_t)=\mathbb{E}_{o(a)}\big[\,|C(H_t)|-|C(H_t\cup\{a,o(a)\})|\,\big],\qquad a\in O_i.
\]
Then distinguish:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Local saturation at scope $i$:}
  \[
  \max_{a\in O_i}\mathrm{EMCG}_i(a\mid F_t)\approx 0.
  \]
  \item \textbf{Field saturation:}
  \[
  \max_i\max_{a\in O_i}\mathrm{EMCG}_i(a\mid F_t)\approx 0.
  \]
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}[Scope-switch recovery]
Local saturation in one scope does not imply field saturation. If
$\max_{a\in O_i}\mathrm{EMCG}_i(a\mid F_t)\approx0$ but
$\max_{a\in O_j}\mathrm{EMCG}_j(a\mid F_t)>0$ for some $j\neq i$, then a policy that permits
scope switching admits continued compression progress without requiring external novelty.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Operator-drift recovery]
For a fixed scope $S_i$ and action $a\in O_i$, one can have
\[
\mathrm{EMCG}_i(a\mid F_t)\approx0\quad\text{and later}\quad
\mathrm{EMCG}_i(a\mid F_{t+\Delta t})>0,
\]
after intermediate work on other scopes updates the operator from $F_t$ to $F_{t+\Delta t}$.
Thus, returns can be structurally informative even when the underlying scope is unchanged.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[BIND-as-progress under long horizons]
For long-horizon scopes ($\tau_i$ large), progress need not require immediate POP. Repeated
constraint-shaping events (BIND/REFUSE/COLLAPSE) can increase continuation quality while the
scope remains unresolved, preserving admissible future work without premature closure.
\end{proposition}

The anti-boredom consequence is scheduling-theoretic: sustained engagement is not the demand
that one process remain perpetually high-gain, but maintenance of a heterogeneous ecology of
unfinished histories whose local gains are sampled across clocks.
