### 2.3 The Invariant-Preservation Theorem (Implicit)

- **Representations**: R: 𝒯 → Obs, where 𝒯 is the transformation group and Obs is the space of observables
- **Preservation Condition**: For all invariants I, I(R(t)(s)) = I(s) for every state s and transformation t
- **Homomorphism Requirement**: R(t₁ ∘ t₂) = R(t₁) ∘ R(t₂)

## 5. Economical Reformulation

### 5.1 Proposed Framework

**Definition (Spherepop Theory, Revised):**

> *Spherepop is the study of algebraic representations that are valid precisely when they constitute a homomorphism from the transformation group 𝒯 preserving a complete set of physical invariants {I₁, I₂, ..., Iₙ} to the space of observable phenomena Obs.*

**Key Improvements:**
1. **Single primitive notion**: Invariant preservation (eliminates circularity)
2. **Clear roles**: Representation is purely descriptive; validity criterion is preservation
3. **Explicit scope**: Only transformations in 𝒯; only invariants in {I₁...Iₙ}
4. **Testability**: Given specific 𝒯 and {Iₖ}, one can verify homomorphism

### 5.2 Formal Axioms (Proposed)

**Axiom 1 (Transformation Group):** 𝒯 is a group under composition with identity element.

**Axiom 2 (Invariant Set):** {I₁, I₂, ..., Iₙ} is a complete set of invariants for the physical domain under study. Completeness means: if two states have identical values for all Iₖ, they are physically indistinguishable.

**Axiom 3 (Representation Validity):** A representation R: 𝒯 → End(S) (where S is the state space) is *valid* iff:
- (a) R is a group homomorphism: R(t₁ ∘ t₂) = R(t₁) ∘ R(t₂)
- (b) For all invariants Iₖ: Iₖ(R(t)(s)) = Iₖ(s) for all t ∈ 𝒯, s ∈ S

**Theorem (Representation Equivalence):** If R₁ and R₂ are both valid representations, they yield identical observable predictions.

*Proof sketch:* By Axiom 2 completeness, states differing only in non-invariant properties are physically identical. Both R₁ and R₂ preserve all invariants by Axiom 3(b), so they map physically indistinguishable states to physically indistinguishable outcomes. □

### 5.3 Advantages of Reformulation

| Issue | Original Framework | Economical Framework |
|-------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Circularity | Reality ↔ Invariants feedback | Linear: Invariants → Preservation → Validity |
| Testability | "Any representation" too broad | Scoped to specific (𝒯, {Iₖ}) pairs |
| Clarity | Dual role of "representation" | Single descriptive role |
| Precision | Vague "geometric facts" | Explicit invariant enumeration |
| Novelty | Unclear vs. gauge theory | Distinguished by completeness criterion |
