The Preservation Hierarchy

\label{sec:ch30-hierarchy}


The argument of this book proceeds by discovering that
each concept introduced is, upon reflection, insufficient
without a higher-order concept. Distinctions require
repair. Repair requires regeneration. Regeneration
requires admissibility. Admissibility requires generativity.

This progression is not arbitrary. It reflects a genuine
hierarchy of preservation — a sequence of increasingly
strong conditions, each strictly stronger than the last.

\begin{definition}{The Preservation Classes}{dist-hierarchy}
  \index{preservation hierarchy|textbf}
  Define the following classes of systems:
  \begin{align*}
    \mathfrak{D} &= \{\text{systems possessing distinctions}\}, \\
    \mathfrak{M} &= \{\text{systems preserving distinctions historically}\}, \\
    \mathfrak{R} &= \{\text{systems capable of repair}\}, \\
    \mathfrak{G} &= \{\text{systems preserving repair capacity (regenerative)}\}, \\
    \mathfrak{A} &= \{\text{systems preserving future reachability (admissible)}\}, \\
    \mathfrak{P} &= \{\text{systems expanding admissible possibility (generative)}\}.
  \end{align*}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}{Preservation Hierarchy Theorem}{preservation-hierarchy}
  \[
    \mathfrak{P}
    \;\subsetneq\;
    \mathfrak{A}
    \;\subsetneq\;
    \mathfrak{G}
    \;\subsetneq\;
    \mathfrak{R}
    \;\subsetneq\;
    \mathfrak{M}
    \;\subsetneq\;
    \mathfrak{D}.
  \]
  Each inclusion is strict.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \textit{Inclusions.}
  Every possibility-expanding system preserves admissible
  future volume, hence $\mathfrak{P} \subseteq \mathfrak{A}$.
  Every admissible system preserves future repair pathways,
  hence $\mathfrak{A} \subseteq \mathfrak{G}$.
  Every regenerative system preserves repair capacity,
  hence $\mathfrak{G} \subseteq \mathfrak{R}$.
  Every repair system must preserve historical information
  (Repair Conservation Law, \cref{thm:repair-conservation}),
  hence $\mathfrak{R} \subseteq \mathfrak{M}$.
  Every memory system necessarily contains distinctions,
  hence $\mathfrak{M} \subseteq \mathfrak{D}$.

  \textit{Strictness.}
  $\mathfrak{M} \subsetneq \mathfrak{D}$:
  a crystal persists and contains distinctions (temperature,
  crystal symmetry) but has no memory in the functional sense.
  $\mathfrak{R} \subsetneq \mathfrak{M}$:
  a read-only archive preserves historical information but
  cannot repair damaged distinctions.
  $\mathfrak{G} \subsetneq \mathfrak{R}$:
  a DNA repair enzyme executes repair but does not
  preserve its own regeneration capacity
  (it is consumed in the process).
  $\mathfrak{A} \subsetneq \mathfrak{G}$:
  a highly adaptive but short-lived organism regenerates
  locally without maintaining admissible volume
  at the population level.
  $\mathfrak{P} \subsetneq \mathfrak{A}$:
  a system may maintain admissible volume ($\Vol(\adm)$
  constant) without expanding it; generativity requires
  strict increase.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}{Structural Dependency Theorem}{structural-dependency}
  The Preservation Hierarchy is not merely a chain of
  strict inclusions but a chain of \emph{dependency}: loss
  of membership in any class forces loss of membership in
  every strictly higher class. Formally, writing
  $\mathfrak{P}\subsetneq\mathfrak{A}\subsetneq\mathfrak{G}
  \subsetneq\mathfrak{R}\subsetneq\mathfrak{M}\subsetneq\mathfrak{D}$
  as before,
  \[
    \mathfrak{D} = \mathbf{0}
    \implies \mathfrak{M} = \mathbf{0}
    \implies \mathfrak{R} = \mathbf{0}
    \implies \mathfrak{G} = \mathbf{0}
    \implies \mathfrak{A} = \mathbf{0}
    \implies \mathfrak{P} = \mathbf{0},
  \]
  where $X = \mathbf{0}$ denotes that system $\Sigma$ exits
  class $X$ (ceases to satisfy its defining condition).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Each implication is the contrapositive of the
  corresponding inclusion established in the Preservation
  Hierarchy Theorem, applied at the level of a single
  system $\Sigma$ rather than at the level of the classes
  themselves.

  If $\Sigma$ exits $\mathfrak{D}$ --- it ceases to possess
  any distinctions --- then by the Axiom of Distinction
  (\cref{axm:dist-axiom}) there is no structure left for a
  memory system to preserve, since memory
  (\cref{defn:memory-defn}) is defined as preservation of
  recoverability \emph{of distinctions}. Hence $\Sigma$
  exits $\mathfrak{M}$.

  If $\Sigma$ exits $\mathfrak{M}$ --- it no longer
  preserves distinctions historically --- then by the
  Repair Conservation Law (\cref{thm:repair-conservation}),
  repair requires operating within the recoverability
  manifold $\mathcal{M}_\reco$ established by historical
  preservation; with no preserved history to repair from,
  the Repair Existence Theorem (\cref{thm:repair-exist})
  cannot be satisfied. Hence $\Sigma$ exits $\mathfrak{R}$.

  If $\Sigma$ exits $\mathfrak{R}$ --- it is no longer
  capable of repair --- then by the Principle of
  Regeneration (\cref{prc:regen-prc}), regeneration is
  preservation of the capacity to perform \emph{future}
  repair; a system incapable of repair at all has no repair
  capacity to preserve. Hence $\Sigma$ exits $\mathfrak{G}$.

  If $\Sigma$ exits $\mathfrak{G}$ --- it is no longer
  regenerative --- then by the Preservation of Possibility
  Theorem (\cref{thm:poss-preserve}), regeneration is
  equivalent to non-decreasing admissible volume
  $\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{P}(x,t)\ge0$; failure of regeneration
  is therefore failure of this admissibility-preservation
  condition. Hence $\Sigma$ exits $\mathfrak{A}$.

  If $\Sigma$ exits $\mathfrak{A}$ --- admissible volume is
  no longer preserved --- then generativity
  (\cref{defn:traj-classes}) requires \emph{strict} increase
  of admissible volume, which is impossible once mere
  preservation already fails. Hence $\Sigma$ exits
  $\mathfrak{P}$.

  Chaining these six implications gives the stated
  result.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}{Foundational Collapse}{foundational-collapse}
  Generativity, admissibility, regeneration, and repair can
  each fail independently while distinction and memory
  persist, but distinction failure is catastrophic: it
  forces the simultaneous failure of every higher layer.
  Formally, $\mathfrak{D}=\mathbf{0} \implies
  \mathfrak{P}=\mathbf{0}$, while none of the converse
  implications hold.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  The forward implication is the composition of all six
  steps in the Structural Dependency Theorem. Failure of
  the converse for each step is exactly the content of the
  strictness clause of the Preservation Hierarchy Theorem:
  the explicit counterexamples given there (the crystal,
  the read-only archive, the DNA repair enzyme, the
  short-lived adaptive organism, the volume-preserving but
  non-expanding system) each exhibit failure at one layer
  of the hierarchy while remaining a member of all strictly
  lower classes, including $\mathfrak{D}$ and $\mathfrak{M}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}{The mathematical backbone}{structural-dependency-remark}
  The Structural Dependency Theorem is the converse
  direction of the Preservation Hierarchy Theorem made
  explicit, and together the two results show that the
  progression
  \[
    \text{Distinction} \to \text{Information} \to \text{Entropy}
    \to \text{History} \to \text{Recoverability} \to \text{Repair}
    \to \text{Regeneration} \to \text{Reachability}
    \to \text{Admissibility}
  \]
  developed across Parts~I--V is not merely sequential
  exposition but a genuinely nested mathematical hierarchy:
  each layer is both necessary for, and strictly weaker
  than, the layer above it. This is the structural backbone
  on which the Generative Admissibility Principle of
  Chapter~\ref{ch:preservation} is built: that principle
  identifies $\mathfrak{P}$, the topmost and most fragile
  class in the hierarchy, as the deepest evaluative
  criterion precisely because the Structural Dependency
  Theorem shows its failure to be the final, and least
  consequential, link in a chain whose earlier links are
  far more catastrophic to lose.
\end{remark}

\noindent
The hierarchy is not a taxonomic curiosity. It is the
formal structure of what it means to be more or less
alive, more or less intelligent, more or less capable
of surviving the future. Every living system,
every institution, every theory, every computation
sits somewhere in this hierarchy. The deepest question
one can ask about any of them is: at what level does it
reside, and is it moving up or down?



The Unified Invariant

\label{sec:ch30-unified}


The preceding parts of the book introduced several
seemingly independent quantities:
entropy $S$, recoverability $\reco$, memory $\mathcal{M}$,
repair capacity $\kappa_\repair$, reachability volume $V_R$,
and admissibility volume $\Vol(\adm)$.

We now show these are not independent. They are all
projections of a single underlying object: the
possibility functional $\mathcal{P}(x,t) = \Vol(\adm(x,t))$.

\begin{theorem}{Preservation Equivalence Theorem}
  {preservation-equivalence}
  Under admissible dynamics, the following quantities
  are monotone together:
  \[
    \mathcal{P}(t),\quad
    V_R(t),\quad
    \kappa_\repair(t),\quad
    \mathcal{M}(t),\quad
    D_\mathcal{E}(t).
  \]
  Each is non-decreasing iff all are non-decreasing.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Admissibility gives $\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{P} \ge 0$.
  Since $\adm(t) \subseteq \reach(t)$, we have
  $\frac{d}{dt}V_R \ge 0$.
  Reachability preserves future reconstruction pathways,
  so $\frac{d}{dt}\kappa_\repair \ge 0$.
  Memory integrates recoverability
  $\mathcal{M}(t) = \int \reco(d,t)\,d\mu_D(d)$,
  so $\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{M} \ge 0$ follows from
  non-decreasing $\kappa_\repair$.
  Memory preserves distinctions, so $D_\mathcal{E}$
  is non-decreasing.
  The converse follows by reversing the chain:
  non-decreasing $D_\mathcal{E}$ implies non-decreasing
  admissible future manifold.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}{Unified Invariant Theorem}{unified-invariant}
  The following quantities are projections of the
  possibility functional $\mathcal{I} = \Vol(\adm)$:
  \begin{align*}
    D_\mathcal{E} &= \Pi_D(\mathcal{I}), &
    \mathcal{M} &= \Pi_M(\mathcal{I}), \\
    \kappa_\repair &= \Pi_\repair(\mathcal{I}), &
    V_R &= \Pi_V(\mathcal{I}), \\
    S_R &= -\log\reco = \Pi_S(\mathcal{I}).
  \end{align*}
  Each is obtained from $\mathcal{I}$ by restriction,
  marginalisation, logarithmic transformation, or
  supremum.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Distinctions determine recoverability via the
  Distinction--Entropy Duality (\cref{law:entropy-dual}).
  Recoverability determines memory
  ($\mathcal{M} = \int\reco\,d\mu_D$).
  Memory determines repair (Repair Existence Theorem,
  \cref{thm:repair-exist}).
  Repair determines reachability (admissible repair
  does not reduce $V_R$, \cref{prop:repair-adm}).
  Reachability determines admissibility
  ($\adm \subseteq \reach$, \cref{defn:adm-manifold}).
  Entropy is the negative log of recoverability
  (\cref{ch:recoverability}).
  Each quantity is therefore obtained from
  $\mathcal{I}$ by a specific transformation.
\end{proof}

\noindent
The Unified Invariant Theorem is not merely a formal
unification. It says something physically and
philosophically significant: all of the quantities
scientists, philosophers, and engineers have introduced
to measure the health of complex systems —
information, entropy, memory, repair capacity,
evolutionary potential, reachability, admissibility —
are different faces of the same geometric object.
That object is future possibility.



The Category of Regenerative Systems

\label{sec:ch30-category}


The Preservation Hierarchy Theorem identifies a class
of systems — $\mathfrak{G}$, regenerative systems —
that is closed under composition. This closure gives
regenerative systems a categorical structure.

\begin{theorem}{Category of Regenerative Systems}
  {regen-category}
  Regenerative systems form a category $\mathbf{Reg}$
  whose objects are distinction ecologies and whose
  morphisms are possibility-preserving maps
  $f: \mathcal{E}_1 \to \mathcal{E}_2$ satisfying
  $\mathcal{P}(f(x)) \ge \mathcal{P}(x)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \textit{Identity}: $\mathrm{id}_\mathcal{E}$ preserves
  $\mathcal{P}$ trivially.

  \textit{Composition}: If $f: \mathcal{E}_1 \to
  \mathcal{E}_2$ and $g: \mathcal{E}_2 \to \mathcal{E}_3$
  are both possibility-preserving, then
  \[
    \mathcal{P}(g(f(x)))
    \ge \mathcal{P}(f(x))
    \ge \mathcal{P}(x),
  \]
  so $g \circ f$ is possibility-preserving.

  \textit{Associativity}: function composition
  is associative.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}{What $\mathbf{Reg}$ captures}{reg-meaning}
  The category $\mathbf{Reg}$ is the correct setting
  for asking questions about the evolution and
  interaction of complex systems.
  Morphisms in $\mathbf{Reg}$ are not arbitrary maps
  between systems. They are maps that respect the
  fundamental conserved quantity: admissible future
  volume.
  A therapy that maps a diseased system to a healthy
  one is a morphism in $\mathbf{Reg}$ iff it preserves
  or expands future possibility.
  A governance reform that maps an extractive
  institution to a generative one is a morphism in
  $\mathbf{Reg}$.
  A scientific revolution that maps a saturated paradigm
  to an enlarged one is a morphism in $\mathbf{Reg}$.
  The realization functors $F_i$ of Appendix~D
  (Chapter~\ref{app:deps}) are all functors
  $F_i: \mathbf{Abs} \to \mathbf{Reg}$ from the
  abstract theorem category into domain-specific
  regenerative systems.
\end{remark}



The Ecology Balance Law

\label{sec:ch30-conservation}


The deepest single equation of the book is not the
RSVP field equation, nor the GAT condition
$\frac{d}{dt}\Vol(\adm) \ge 0$.
It is the balance equation that governs how admissible
future volume changes.

\begin{theorem}{Ecology of Distinctions Balance Law}
  {ecology-final}
  \index{ecology!ecology of distinctions|textbf}
  For any distinction ecology $\mathcal{E}$:
  \[
    \frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{P}(t)
    \;=\;
    \mathcal{G}(t) + \mathcal{R}(t)
    - \mathcal{L}(t) - \mathcal{X}(t),
  \]
  where:
  \begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item $\mathcal{G}$: rate of new admissible
          distinction generation.
    \item $\mathcal{R}$: rate of admissible repair
          (restoration of damaged
          distinctions).\index{repair!conservation}
    \item $\mathcal{L}$: rate of distinction loss
          through entropy and projection failure.
    \item $\mathcal{X}$: extractive contraction
          (trajectories that consume future possibility
          for present gain).
  \end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  All changes to admissible future volume arise from
  exactly four sources.
  Creation of new distinctions that are admissible
  enlarges $\adm$: contributes $+\mathcal{G}$.
  Repair restores formerly admissible distinctions
  to the admissibility manifold: contributes $+\mathcal{R}$.
  Entropy growth and projection failure remove recoverable
  distinctions from the admissibility manifold:
  contributes $-\mathcal{L}$.
  Extractive trajectories consume the distinction
  structures that sustained admissibility, producing
  a net reduction in $\Vol(\adm)$ even when the system
  continues: contributes $-\mathcal{X}$.
  These four contributions are additive and exhaustive,
  giving the stated equation.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}{A balance equation, not a conservation law}
  {balance-vs-conservation}
  The name ``Balance Law'' is deliberate. A conservation law
  in the physical sense identifies a quantity $\mathcal{C}$
  such that $d\mathcal{C}/dt = 0$ --- the quantity neither
  grows nor decays. The present equation says something
  different: it specifies how $\mathcal{P}$ \emph{changes}
  as a function of four rate terms. This is a continuity
  or balance equation, structurally analogous to the
  continuity equation in fluid dynamics or the accounting
  identities of national income analysis. The earlier
  ``Memory Conservation Law'' and ``Repair Conservation
  Law'' in this book use ``conservation'' in a different
  and legitimate sense: they assert that certain
  quantities are preserved (non-decreasing) under
  admissible operations. The present equation does not
  assert preservation; it asserts the complete accounting
  of changes. Both uses are standard; they are now
  explicitly distinguished.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}{Regime Classification}{ecology-regime}
  A distinction ecology is:
  \begin{align*}
    \text{regenerative} &\iff
    \mathcal{G}+\mathcal{R} > \mathcal{L}+\mathcal{X}, \\
    \text{stable}       &\iff
    \mathcal{G}+\mathcal{R} = \mathcal{L}+\mathcal{X}, \\
    \text{collapsing}   &\iff
    \mathcal{G}+\mathcal{R} < \mathcal{L}+\mathcal{X}.
  \end{align*}
\end{corollary}

\noindent
The Balance Law is the formal counterpart of the
biological truism that life maintains local order by
exporting entropy. But it goes further. Life does not
merely export entropy --- it generates new distinctions
faster than entropy erodes them, repairs damaged
distinctions faster than they degrade, and resists
extractive pressures that would consume its future.
Regenerative systems satisfy $\mathcal{G} + \mathcal{R}
> \mathcal{L} + \mathcal{X}$. Everything else follows.

What Is Meant by ``Functor''

\label{sec:ch30-functor-meaning}


A careful reader trained in category theory will ask
whether the thirteen mappings above are genuine functors
in the technical sense, or whether ``functor'' is being
used loosely to mean ``analogy'' or ``correspondence.''
The question is fair and deserves a direct answer.

A functor $F : \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}$ in the
categorical sense requires:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item objects of $\mathcal{C}$ map to objects of
        $\mathcal{D}$;
  \item morphisms of $\mathcal{C}$ map to morphisms of
        $\mathcal{D}$;
  \item composition is preserved: $F(g \circ f) =
        F(g) \circ F(f)$; and
  \item identities are preserved: $F(\mathrm{id}_X) =
        \mathrm{id}_{F(X)}$.
\end{enumerate}
The table above supplies (i) and (ii) for each realization:
distinctions map to domain-specific distinction-like
quantities, and logical relationships between theorems map
to domain-specific correspondences. Conditions (iii) and
(iv) are satisfied at the level of the logical structure
of the abstract category $\mathbf{Abs}$: because the
morphisms in $\mathbf{Abs}$ are logical implications
between theorems, composition is provability-preserving
by construction, and identity morphisms are tautologies.

\begin{example}{Explicit Functoriality: Flow Computing}
  {functor-flow-explicit}
  Let $\mathbf{Abs}$ be the abstract theorem category with
  objects $\{\text{Distinction}, \text{Recoverability},
  \text{Repair}, \text{Admissibility}\}$ and morphisms the
  implications proved in Parts~I--V. Let $\mathbf{Flow}$
  be the category of pipeline stages with objects
  $\{D_F(\delta), \kappa_F, V_R^F, \adm_F\}$ and morphisms
  the stage-composition maps $\delta_i \circ \delta_j$.
  The functor $F_\mathrm{Flow}$ assigns:
  \begin{align*}
    \text{Distinction} &\mapsto D_F(\delta)
      && \text{(distinction capacity of a stage)} \\
    \text{Recoverability} &\mapsto \kappa_F
      && \text{(residual capacity after lossy stage)} \\
    \text{Repair} &\mapsto \text{lossless upstream stage}
      && \text{(corrective reinsertion of distinctions)} \\
    \text{Admissibility} &\mapsto
      \text{Pipeline Determination Theorem condition}
  \end{align*}
  Composition is preserved because pipeline composition
  is associative and the Pipeline Determination Theorem
  holds for any composition of stages. Identity is the
  identity stage $\delta_\mathrm{id}$ that passes all
  distinctions unchanged.
\end{example}

The other twelve realizations satisfy the same conditions
at the same level of detail, though a full categorical
treatment of each — specifying all objects, morphisms,
naturality conditions, and isomorphisms — lies beyond the
scope of this book. What can be claimed honestly is this:
these are not mere analogies. The formal structure is the
same in each domain, the mapping is explicit, and
functoriality holds in the sense that the abstract
logical relationships are preserved under the mapping.
Whether each realization satisfies stronger categorical
properties (e.g.\ constituting an equivalence of categories,
or admitting natural transformations between different
domain realizations) is a programme for future work, not
a claim already established here.

\begin{remark}{Structural Homology vs.\ Full Functoriality}
  {structural-homology}
  Readers who find ``functor'' too strong a term are
  invited to read the thirteen mappings as instances of
  \emph{structural realization}: the abstract theorem
  category $\mathbf{Abs}$ is realized in each domain
  in the way that a group can be realized by a group of
  symmetries, or a topological space can be realized by a
  point-set. The realization need not be an equivalence of
  categories to constitute a genuine and non-trivial
  unification. What it must do --- and what each of the
  thirteen mappings does --- is preserve the inferential
  structure: if Theorem~A implies Theorem~B in $\mathbf{Abs}$,
  then $F(A)$ implies $F(B)$ in the domain.
\end{remark}

The Transition to Chapter 31

\label{sec:ch30-transition}


The present chapter has shown that the framework is
unified: all its concepts are projections of a single
invariant, all its domains are functors into the same
category, and all its dynamical equations are
special cases of one conservation law.

But there remains something the conservation law
does not address.

The conservation law tells us how $\mathcal{P}(t)$
changes. It does not tell us why preserving
$\mathcal{P}$ matters.

The Future Distinction Optimality Theorem proved that
generative trajectories are \emph{strategically}
dominant. But strategic dominance is not the same
as value. An agent with an extremely short time horizon
may rationally prefer an extractive trajectory.
A culture that does not care about future generations
may rationally pursue pathological continuation.

Chapter~\ref{ch:preservation} addresses this.
It asks not how $\mathcal{P}$ changes but what it
would mean for the change to matter. It proves the
strongest theorem in the book: that admissible
future volume is not merely a useful quantity but
the correct invariant for evaluating any system
whose existence depends on the continuation of
distinction-production.

That proof is the subject of the final chapter.


Related Frameworks

\label{sec:ch30-related-frameworks}

\subsection*{Applied Category Theory: Fong and Spivak}

\emph{Convergence.} Fong and Spivak's applied category theory
treats category theory specifically as a unifying language for
compositional structure across disparate scientific and
engineering domains: wiring diagrams, network models, and systems
from electrical engineering to database theory are each shown to
instantiate the same categorical patterns \parencite{fong2019}.
This is exactly the methodological move made above with the
Realization Functor (\cref{defn:realization-functor}): each of
the thirteen realizations in Parts~VI--IX is presented not as an
analogy but as a functor $F_i:\mathbf{Abs}\to\mathbf{Dom}_i$,
preserving the actual logical structure of the abstract theory
rather than merely echoing its vocabulary in a new domain.

\emph{Divergence.} Applied category theory's compositional
diagrams are typically built up from elementary categorical
primitives --- monoidal categories, operads, wiring diagrams ---
chosen in advance for their generality and reusability, with the
specific application supplied afterward. The category
$\mathbf{Reg}$ (\cref{thm:regen-category}) and its functors $F_i$
are constructed the opposite way: $\mathbf{Reg}$ is built from the
specific theorems already proved in Parts~I--V, and each $F_i$ is
verified against the actual content of a specific chapter rather
than chosen for general applicability in advance. The
compositionality here is a discovered fact about an
already-existing body of theorems, not a designed feature of the
categorical apparatus itself.

\emph{Open question.} Whether the thirteen realization functors
compose with one another in the applied-category-theory sense ---
whether two realizations can be combined via a categorical product
or pushout to yield a coherent joint realization, the way
Fong--Spivak-style wiring diagrams compose --- or whether they are
better understood as thirteen independent functors sharing only a
common domain, with no natural composition among them, is not
explored here.

\section*{Chapter Summary}
\begin{chapsummary}
  \item The argument of the book is a single progression:
        Distinction $\to$ Information $\to$ Entropy
        $\to$ History $\to$ Recoverability $\to$ Repair
        $\to$ Regeneration $\to$ Reachability
        $\to$ Admissibility $\to$ Generativity.
  \item Systems are ranked by a strict hierarchy
        $\mathfrak{P} \subsetneq \mathfrak{A}
        \subsetneq \mathfrak{G} \subsetneq \mathfrak{R}
        \subsetneq \mathfrak{M} \subsetneq \mathfrak{D}$
        (\cref{thm:preservation-hierarchy}), and loss of
        any layer forces loss of every strictly higher
        layer, with loss of distinction itself the most
        catastrophic (\cref{thm:structural-dependency,cor:foundational-collapse}).
  \item All major quantities — entropy, recoverability,
        memory, repair capacity, reachability, admissibility
        — are projections of the single invariant
        $\mathcal{I} = \Vol(\adm)$
        (\cref{thm:unified-invariant}).
  \item Regenerative systems form a category
        $\mathbf{Reg}$ under possibility-preserving maps
        (\cref{thm:regen-category}).
  \item The dynamics of all distinction ecologies are
        governed by:
        $\dot{\mathcal{P}} = \mathcal{G} + \mathcal{R}
        - \mathcal{L} - \mathcal{X}$
        (\cref{thm:ecology-final}).
  \item Each realization in Parts~VI--IX is a functor
        $F_i: \mathbf{Abs} \to \mathbf{Dom}_i$ that
        instantiates the abstract theorem structure
        in a domain-specific setting.
  \item The book has fully proved the structural spine;
        several realization theorems are sketches
        requiring additional assumptions.
  \item What remains for Chapter~\ref{ch:preservation}:
        not how $\mathcal{P}$ changes but why its
        preservation constitutes a value, not merely
        a strategy.
\end{chapsummary}

\end{epigraph}


Introduction

\label{sec:ch31-intro}

The preceding chapters developed a progression of concepts
that initially appeared independent. Distinction produced
information. Information implied entropy. Histories
replaced states as the primary objects of analysis.
Recoverability quantified the persistence of historical
structure. Memory preserved recoverability. Repair opposed
entropic degradation. Regeneration preserved repair itself.
Reachability quantified future possibility. Admissibility
distinguished futures that preserved future possibility
from those that merely continued existing.

At each stage a familiar explanatory strategy was shown to
be incomplete. Objects required distinctions. Distinctions
required histories. Histories required recoverability.
Recoverability required repair. Repair required
regeneration. Regeneration required admissibility.

The present chapter demonstrates that this sequence is not
merely pedagogical. It is the manifestation of a single
underlying invariant.

The central claim of this chapter is that the deepest
conserved quantity is neither matter, energy, information,
complexity, intelligence, nor even continuation itself. The
deepest conserved quantity is possibility. More precisely,
the fundamental quantity preserved by generatively
admissible dynamics is the capacity of a system to generate
future distinctions.

The entire monograph may therefore be interpreted as the
progressive discovery of the geometric conditions under
which possibility survives. The goal of this chapter is to
make that statement precise, and to show that it subsumes
the Preservation Hierarchy Theorem of
Chapter~\ref{ch:ecology-synthesis} and the Structural Dependency
Theorem proved there
(\cref{thm:preservation-hierarchy,thm:structural-dependency})
as instances of a single functional inequality.


The Possibility Functional

\label{sec:ch31-functional}

Let $\mathcal{A}(x)$ denote the admissible future manifold
associated with state $x$, and recall from the Future
Volume Theorem of Chapter~\ref{ch:reachability} that
\[
  V_F(x) = \Vol\bigl(\mathcal{A}(x)\bigr)
\]
measures the accessible admissible future of a system. The
possibility functional $\mathcal{P}(x,t)=\Vol(\adm(x,t))$
already introduced in earlier chapters measures this volume
directly; what it does not capture is the \emph{diversity}
of the futures it counts. Volume alone is insufficient. A
large future volume containing only mutually equivalent
trajectories does not preserve genuine possibility. We
therefore refine the possibility functional to incorporate
diversity explicitly.

\begin{definition}{Admissible Distinction Entropy}{adm-dist-entropy}
  Let $\{p_i\}$ be the distribution of distinguishable
  trajectory classes within $\mathcal{A}(x)$. Define
  \[
    S_A(x) = -\sum_i p_i \log p_i.
  \]
  \index{admissible distinction entropy|textbf}
  \index{distinction!entropy|textbf}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}{Refined Possibility Functional}{refined-possibility}
  The \emph{refined possibility functional} of a state $x$
  is
  \[
    \Pi(x) = \Vol\bigl(\mathcal{A}(x)\bigr)\, S_A(x)
           = V_F(x)\, S_A(x).
  \]
  \index{possibility functional!refined|textbf}
\end{definition}

Future volume without diversity yields fragility: a system
with large $V_F$ but $S_A\approx0$ has many reachable
futures that are effectively redundant, and the loss of any
single pathway is immaterial only because the rest are
copies of it. Diversity without volume yields stagnation: a
system with large $S_A$ but small $V_F$ has few futures to
be diverse among. Both are required, and $\Pi$ is the
multiplicative combination that vanishes whenever either
factor vanishes.

\begin{remark}{Relation to the unrefined functional}{pi-vs-P}
  $\Pi$ refines, rather than replaces, the possibility
  functional $\mathcal{P}=\Vol(\adm)$ used throughout
  Chapters~\ref{ch:reachability}--\ref{ch:ecology-synthesis}.
  Where $S_A(x)$ is approximately constant across the
  trajectories under comparison, $\Pi$ and $\mathcal{P}$
  induce the same ordering and every theorem stated in
  terms of $\mathcal{P}$ continues to hold for $\Pi$ in
  place of $\mathcal{P}$. Where $S_A$ varies, $\Pi$ is the
  strictly more discriminating quantity, since it is
  sensitive to qualitative diversity of futures and not
  merely their aggregate measure.
\end{remark}


Preservation Dynamics

\label{sec:ch31-dynamics}

Consider an admissible trajectory $\gamma(t)$, and write
$V_F(t) = V_F(\gamma(t))$, $S_A(t) = S_A(\gamma(t))$,
$\Pi(t) = \Pi(\gamma(t))$. Differentiating the product
defining $\Pi$ gives

\begin{theorem}{Possibility Decomposition Theorem}{possibility-decomposition}
  Along any admissible trajectory,
  \[
    \frac{d\Pi}{dt}
    = S_A \frac{dV_F}{dt} + V_F \frac{dS_A}{dt}.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Immediate from the product rule applied to
  $\Pi(t)=V_F(t)S_A(t)$ (\cref{defn:refined-possibility}).
\end{proof}

The first term measures expansion or contraction of future
accessibility; the second measures expansion or contraction
of future diversity. This decomposition motivates a
threefold classification of dynamical systems.

\begin{definition}{Possibility Classes}{possibility-classes}
  A trajectory $\gamma$ is:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{generative} if $\frac{d\Pi}{dt} > 0$;
    \item \emph{extractive} if $\frac{d\Pi}{dt} < 0$;
    \item \emph{neutral} if $\frac{d\Pi}{dt} = 0$.
  \end{itemize}
  \index{possibility!classes of trajectories}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}{Consistency with earlier usage}{possibility-classes-consistency}
  This classification is consistent with, and sharpens, the
  generative/extractive distinction already used informally
  in Chapters~\ref{ch:reachability}--\ref{ch:ecology-synthesis}
  and formalized for the unrefined functional $\mathcal{P}$
  in the Generative Admissibility Theorem
  (\cref{thm:gat-final}). \cref{thm:gat-final} characterizes
  generativity by $\frac{d}{dt}\Vol(\adm)\ge0$; the
  Possibility Classes definition strengthens this to account
  for diversity via $S_A$, and the two coincide exactly when
  $S_A$ is held fixed along the comparison, by
  \cref{rmk:pi-vs-P}.
\end{remark}


The Preservation Implication Theorem

\label{sec:ch31-implication}

The progression developed throughout the book may now be
formalized as a chain of logical implications, complementing
the class-inclusion form of the Preservation Hierarchy
Theorem proved in Chapter~\ref{ch:ecology-synthesis}
(\cref{thm:preservation-hierarchy}).

\begin{theorem}{Preservation Implication Theorem}{preservation-implication}
  For every system $\mathcal{E}$,
  \[
    \text{Generativity} \implies \text{Admissibility}
    \implies \text{Regeneration} \implies \text{Repair}
    \implies \text{Continuation}.
  \]
  Furthermore, all implications are strict.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Generativity requires preservation of future possibility:
  by \cref{defn:possibility-classes}, a generative
  trajectory has $d\Pi/dt > 0$, hence in particular
  $dV_F/dt \ge 0$ whenever $S_A>0$ stays bounded, by
  \cref{thm:possibility-decomposition}. Future possibility
  cannot be preserved unless admissible futures remain
  accessible; therefore generativity implies admissibility,
  in the sense of $\mathfrak{P}\subseteq\mathfrak{A}$
  already established directly in the Preservation
  Hierarchy Theorem (\cref{thm:preservation-hierarchy}).

  Admissibility requires maintenance of structures capable
  of generating future admissible states: this is
  regeneration, by \cref{thm:regeneration}. Hence
  admissibility implies regeneration
  ($\mathfrak{A}\subseteq\mathfrak{G}$).

  Regeneration requires preservation of repair capacity, by
  the Principle of Regeneration (\cref{prc:regen-prc}).
  Therefore regeneration implies repair
  ($\mathfrak{G}\subseteq\mathfrak{R}$).

  Repair prevents destructive collapse of recoverable
  structure and therefore implies continuation of the
  system's distinctions, by the Repair Conservation Law
  (\cref{thm:repair-conservation}).

  Strictness is exactly the strictness already proved for
  each corresponding inclusion in the Preservation Hierarchy
  Theorem (\cref{thm:preservation-hierarchy}): a crystal
  continues without repairing; a thermostat repairs without
  regenerating; an immune system regenerates without
  preserving all admissible futures; a stable but
  possibility-narrowing institution preserves admissibility
  of selected trajectories while reducing possibility
  volume, by \cref{defn:possibility-classes}. Each
  implication is therefore proper.
\end{proof}
