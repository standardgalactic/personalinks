The Reachability Bridge

\label{sec:ch16-bridge}

The next theorem is the central bridge between RSVP and the
geometric machinery of Part~V.

\begin{theorem}{Reachability Capacity Theorem}{rsvp-reachability}
  Reachability volume satisfies
  \[
    V_R(x,t) \propto \Phi(x,t)\, e^{-S(x,t)}.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Increasing capacity $\Phi$ enlarges the set of reachable
  futures: more stored distinction-producing capacity
  permits more admissible trajectories. Increasing
  constraint $S$ reduces reachable futures, by the
  Constraint Volume Theorem (\cref{thm:constraint-vol}).
  By Chapter~13, $V_R$ must therefore increase monotonically
  with $\Phi$ and decrease monotonically with $S$. Among
  functional forms satisfying both monotonicity conditions
  together with constraint additivity --- the requirement
  that two independent regions of constraint $S_1, S_2$
  combine as $S_1 + S_2$, producing a multiplicative
  reduction $e^{-S_1}e^{-S_2} = e^{-(S_1+S_2)}$ in
  reachable volume --- the exponential form
  \[
    V_R = K\, \Phi\, e^{-S}
  \]
  is the \emph{simplest member} satisfying these
  conditions. Functions of the form
  $f(S) = e^{-\lambda S}$ for any $\lambda > 0$ form a
  family, all satisfying additivity; more complex forms
  (e.g.\ $e^{-S^2}$ under an appropriate composition rule)
  are also conceivable. The exponential form with
  $\lambda = 1$ is adopted as the working hypothesis of
  the RSVP framework: simple, analytically tractable,
  and consistent with the constraint-additivity
  requirement. Whether it is the \emph{unique} such form
  depends on additional axioms not imposed here; the
  claim of uniqueness is withdrawn in favour of the
  weaker claim that this is a natural and minimal choice.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}{The most important bridge}{rsvp-bridge-remark}
  This theorem is arguably the single most important
  connection between RSVP and the admissibility programme
  of Part~V. It converts every reachability and
  admissibility result of Chapters~13--15 into a statement
  about the physical fields $\Phi$ and $S$.
\end{remark}

  \[

    \qquad

    \qquad
    \frac{\delta\mathcal{A}}{\delta S} = 0.

\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]

\end{proof}


Lamphrodyne Relaxation

\label{sec:ch16-lamphrodyne}

\begin{definition}{Lamphrodyne Relaxation}{lamphrodyne-relaxation}
  A trajectory obeys \emph{lamphrodyne relaxation} if
  \[
    \frac{d\Phi}{dt}
    = -\mu \frac{\partial S}{\partial \Phi}.
  \]
  \index{lamphrodyne relaxation|textbf}
\end{definition}

This gives the geometric mechanism that drives systems
toward admissible regions: capacity flows so as to reduce
the local marginal constraint.

\begin{theorem}{Lamphrodyne Stability Theorem}{lamphrodyne}
  Along a lamphrodyne trajectory,
  \[
    \frac{dS}{dt} \le 0.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Substituting $\frac{d\Phi}{dt} = -\mu\,\partial S/\partial\Phi$
  into the chain rule,
  \[
    \frac{dS}{dt}
    = \frac{\partial S}{\partial \Phi}\frac{d\Phi}{dt}
    = -\mu \left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial \Phi}\right)^{\!2}
    \le 0. \qedhere
  \]
\end{proof}

This is essentially the RSVP analogue of gradient descent
in constraint space, and it furnishes the dynamical
mechanism underlying gravitational attraction in
Chapter~\ref{ch:gravity}.

Existence, Stability, and Attractors

\label{sec:ch16-existence}

The preceding sections established the RSVP field equations
and their connection to reachability and admissibility. What
they have not yet established is whether the coupled system
$(\Phi,\mathbf{v},S)$ actually admits well-behaved solutions,
and whether those solutions settle into recognisable
long-term regimes. This section supplies that missing layer:
a local well-posedness result, an energy functional with a
Lyapunov-type decay property, and a characterisation of RSVP
attractors.

\begin{definition}{RSVP Energy Functional}{rsvp-energy}
  For a domain $\Omega\subseteq X$, define
  \[
    E[\Phi,\mathbf{v},S]
    = \int_\Omega \left(
        \alpha|\nabla\Phi|^2
        + \beta|\mathbf{v}|^2
        + \gamma S^2
      \right) dV,
    \qquad \alpha,\beta,\gamma > 0.
  \]
  \index{RSVP!energy functional|textbf}
\end{definition}

The three terms penalise, respectively, sharp spatial
variation in capacity, kinetic transport, and constraint
magnitude. $E$ is non-negative and vanishes only on the
trivial configuration $\nabla\Phi=\mathbf{v}=S=0$.

\begin{theorem}{Local Well-Posedness Theorem}{rsvp-well-posed}
  Let $\Omega\subseteq X$ be bounded with smooth boundary,
  and let $(\Phi_0,\mathbf{v}_0,S_0)$ be initial data with
  $E[\Phi_0,\mathbf{v}_0,S_0] < \infty$. Then the coupled
  system consisting of the Capacity Conservation Theorem
  (\cref{thm:capacity-conservation}), the Constraint
  Accumulation Theorem
  (\cref{thm:constraint-accumulation-rsvp}), and lamphrodyne
  relaxation (\cref{defn:lamphrodyne-relaxation}) admits a
  unique solution $(\Phi,\mathbf{v},S)$ on some maximal
  interval $[0,T_{\max})$, depending continuously on the
  initial data.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
  The continuity equation for $\Phi$ and the
  reaction--diffusion equation for $S$ are both
  quasilinear parabolic-type equations once
  $\mathbf{v}$ is treated as a given coefficient field;
  standard fixed-point arguments (Banach fixed point on a
  short time interval, using the finiteness of
  $E[\Phi_0,\mathbf{v}_0,S_0]$ to bound the relevant
  Sobolev norms) give local existence and uniqueness for
  $\Phi$ and $S$ given $\mathbf{v}$. Substituting back into
  the Euler--Lagrange equation for $\mathbf{v}$
  (\cref{thm:rsvp-el}) and iterating the fixed point over
  the triple $(\Phi,\mathbf{v},S)$ yields a contraction on a
  sufficiently short time interval, by finiteness of $E$ and
  boundedness of $\Omega$. Continuous dependence on initial
  data follows from a standard Grönwall estimate applied to
  the difference of two solutions.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}{Global existence is not claimed}{rsvp-global-caveat}
  The theorem is deliberately local. Whether $T_{\max}=\infty$
  in general --- global well-posedness --- depends on
  whether $E$ remains bounded along the flow, which is
  precisely what the Lyapunov argument below addresses under
  the additional hypothesis of admissibility.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}{Energy Decay Theorem}{rsvp-energy-decay}
  Along any trajectory obeying lamphrodyne relaxation
  (\cref{defn:lamphrodyne-relaxation}) with admissible
  dynamics, the energy functional is non-increasing:
  \[
    \frac{dE}{dt} \le 0.
  \]
  \index{Lyapunov function|textbf}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Differentiating $E$ under the integral sign and applying
  the Capacity Conservation and Constraint Accumulation
  equations gives
  \[
    \frac{dE}{dt}
    = \int_\Omega \left(
        2\alpha\,\nabla\Phi\cdot\nabla\dot\Phi
        + 2\beta\,\mathbf{v}\cdot\dot{\mathbf{v}}
        + 2\gamma S\dot S
      \right) dV.
  \]
  By the Lamphrodyne Stability Theorem
  (\cref{thm:lamphrodyne}), $\dot S$ is driven by
  $-\mu(\partial S/\partial\Phi)^2 \le 0$ along the
  relaxation flow, and the transport term
  $\mathbf{v}\cdot\dot{\mathbf{v}}$ is controlled by the
  dissipative part of the Euler--Lagrange equations
  (\cref{thm:rsvp-el}), which by construction extremises
  $\mathcal{A}$ subject to non-increasing kinetic
  contribution under admissible boundary conditions.
  Integrating by parts on the $\nabla\Phi\cdot\nabla\dot\Phi$
  term and using that boundary flux vanishes for admissible
  trajectories (no capacity is injected at $\partial\Omega$)
  leaves a sum of non-positive terms, giving
  $dE/dt \le 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}{Global Existence Under Bounded Energy}
  {rsvp-global-exist}
  If $E[\Phi_0,\mathbf{v}_0,S_0] < \infty$ and the
  trajectory remains admissible, then $T_{\max} = \infty$:
  the local solution of \cref{thm:rsvp-well-posed} extends
  globally in time.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  By \cref{thm:rsvp-energy-decay}, $E$ is non-increasing and
  bounded below by $0$, hence bounded on
  $[0,T_{\max})$. Standard continuation criteria for
  quasilinear parabolic systems state that a local solution
  fails to extend only if the relevant energy norm blows up
  in finite time; since $E$ remains bounded, no blow-up
  occurs, and the solution extends to all $t \ge 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}{RSVP Attractor}{rsvp-attractor}
  An \emph{RSVP attractor} is a non-empty, closed, invariant
  set $\mathcal{O}\subseteq\{(\Phi,\mathbf{v},S)\}$ of the
  field flow such that for every trajectory with bounded
  initial energy, $\mathrm{dist}\bigl((\Phi(t),\mathbf{v}(t),S(t)),\mathcal{O}\bigr)\to0$
  as $t\to\infty$.
  \index{RSVP!attractor|textbf}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}{Attractor Convergence Theorem}{rsvp-attractor-conv}
  Under the hypotheses of \cref{cor:rsvp-global-exist} and
  bounded entropy production
  ($\int_0^\infty |\dot S|\,dt < \infty$), every admissible
  RSVP trajectory converges to an attractor class
  $\mathcal{O}$ on which $E$ is constant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  By \cref{thm:rsvp-energy-decay}, $E(t)$ is non-increasing
  and bounded below, hence converges to a limit
  $E_\infty \ge 0$. Bounded entropy production implies $S(t)$
  converges (as a non-increasing-variation function bounded
  below), and the Lamphrodyne Stability Theorem
  (\cref{thm:lamphrodyne}) forces
  $\partial S/\partial\Phi \to 0$ along the flow, so the
  driving term for further change in $\Phi$ vanishes in the
  limit. The omega-limit set of any trajectory with these
  properties is therefore a non-empty, closed, invariant set
  on which $E \equiv E_\infty$ --- by definition, an
  attractor $\mathcal{O}$. Standard LaSalle-invariance-type
  argument (applicable here because $E$ is a genuine
  Lyapunov function by \cref{thm:rsvp-energy-decay}) gives
  convergence of the trajectory itself to $\mathcal{O}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}{Why this matters}{rsvp-attractor-remark}
  The Attractor Convergence Theorem upgrades RSVP from a
  system of suggestive field equations to a genuine field
  theory with a well-posedness and long-time-behaviour
  package: solutions exist, are unique, depend continuously
  on data, and --- under the physically motivated hypothesis
  of bounded entropy production --- settle into classifiable
  attractor regimes. Chapters~\ref{ch:gravity}--\ref{ch:cosmo-completion}
  may therefore treat gravitational and cosmological
  structures as RSVP attractors without further
  justification of existence.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Dynamical Systems Theory}

\emph{Convergence.} The Attractor Convergence and Lamphrodyne
Stability Theorems above already deploy the technical apparatus
of classical dynamical systems theory directly --- Lyapunov
functions, LaSalle invariance, omega-limit sets, attractors
\parencite{strogatz2015,smale1967}. This is not analogy: the
proof of \cref{thm:rsvp-attractor-conv} is a direct application
of LaSalle's invariance principle, the same tool used throughout
nonlinear dynamics to establish convergence of dissipative
systems to invariant sets.

\emph{Divergence.} Classical dynamical systems theory studies
abstract phase-space flows whose physical content, if any, is
supplied externally and after the fact --- a Lorenz attractor's
variables mean whatever a modeller decides they mean. The
Three-Field Necessity Theorem (\cref{thm:three-field-necessity})
instead derives the field content itself --- capacity $\Phi$,
transport $v$, constraint $S$ --- as the unique minimal
structure able to realise a reachability geometry
(Chapter~\ref{ch:reachability}) at all. The dynamics here is not
chosen freely and interpreted afterward; it is constrained from
the start by what a reachability-respecting field theory
requires.

\emph{Open question.} Whether RSVP's attractor classes
$\mathcal{O}$ correspond to the standard taxonomy of
dynamical-systems attractors --- fixed points, limit cycles,
strange attractors --- or constitute a genuinely new attractor
type specific to admissibility-constrained field dynamics, is
not worked out here.

RSVP as Interpretation and Realization

\label{sec:ch16-rsvp-status}

A reader trained in physical theory will ask a question
this chapter has not yet addressed directly: what is the
empirical status of RSVP?

The distinction-repair-reachability framework motivates
three fields $(\Phi, \mathbf{v}, S)$ on the grounds that
something carrying capacity, something transporting it, and
something accumulating constraint \emph{must} exist in any
physical realization of the abstract framework. This is
a philosophical argument for the existence of the formal
structure, not an empirical derivation of specific field
content. The Standard Model of particle physics has a
well-established field content --- gauge bosons, fermions,
the Higgs field --- with extensive empirical confirmation.
This chapter does not claim that $(\Phi, \mathbf{v}, S)$
reduces to Standard Model fields, nor that RSVP makes novel
empirical predictions distinguishable from standard physics.

\begin{remark}{The Epistemic Status of RSVP}
  {rsvp-epistemic-status}
  RSVP should presently be understood in one of two
  ways, depending on the reader's purposes:
  \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
    \item \emph{As a realization framework.} RSVP supplies
          a geometric language --- distinction capacity,
          transport, constraint accumulation --- in which the
          abstract theorems of Parts~I--V acquire a
          field-theoretic form. It is a physical
          \emph{interpretation} of the framework, in the
          same sense that statistical mechanics is an
          interpretation of thermodynamics: it provides a
          level of description at which the abstract
          quantities get explicit physical meaning without
          claiming to replace the underlying microphysics.
    \item \emph{As a speculative physical theory.} If one
          takes the three-field content as a genuine
          proposal about fundamental physics, then RSVP
          makes predictions. The Reachability Capacity
          Theorem implies that regions of high $S$ are
          gravitationally active; the RSVP Admissibility
          Theorem implies that entropy-generating processes
          suppress future reachability. Whether these
          predictions are empirically distinguishable from
          general relativity and thermodynamics in any
          accessible regime is an open question the
          chapter does not settle.
  \end{enumerate}
  Nothing in this book depends on resolving (i) versus (ii).
  The formal framework is valid under interpretation (i)
  regardless of whether (ii) is correct.
\end{remark}

The chapters that follow --- Gravity, Expyrotic Cosmology,
Cosmological Completion --- proceed under interpretation
(i): they show how gravitational and cosmological
structures \emph{can be read} as realizations of the
abstract distinction-theoretic framework. This is a
genuine and non-trivial claim. It is not the same as
claiming that distinction theory \emph{derives} gravity
from first principles, a claim that would require showing
that the RSVP field equations are the uniquely correct
microphysical description. That stronger claim is left as
an open research question (\cref{app:open-problems}).

From Capacity to Potential

\label{sec:ch17-potential}

Chapter~\ref{ch:rsvp} established that a physical realization
of reachability geometry requires three fields: capacity,
transport, and constraint. This chapter asks what becomes of
ordinary gravitational phenomena once the capacity field
$\Phi(x,t)$ is taken to be physically real rather than merely
a bookkeeping device for distinction-producing potential. The
answer developed below is that gravity need not be introduced
as an additional postulate at all; it falls out of capacity
gradients already implied by Chapter~\ref{ch:rsvp}'s field
content.

General relativity takes a different route to the same
phenomena. Its central postulate, the Einstein field
equations, couples spacetime curvature directly to the
stress-energy tensor $T_{\mu\nu}$: mass and energy are
posited as the source of curvature, and the equivalence
principle is introduced separately to explain why
gravitational and inertial mass coincide
\parencite{misner1973}. This is an extremely successful
postulate, confirmed to extraordinary precision, and Chapter~17
does not compete with General Relativity on its own
empirical terms. It is, however, a postulate: nothing internal
to general relativity explains why mass-energy specifically
should be the quantity sourcing curvature, as opposed to some
other conserved quantity. Within the architecture of this
book, gravity emerges as a consequence of gradients in
distinction-supporting capacity rather than being introduced
as a fundamental force, and the question general relativity
answers by postulate --- why does \emph{this} quantity source
curvature? --- is answered here by derivation: capacity is
what sources gravitational behaviour because capacity is what
a distinction-theoretic universe has to move toward reachable
futures.

The conceptual transition is
\[
  \text{Distinction} \to \text{Capacity} \to \text{Reachability}
  \to \text{Capacity Gradient} \to \text{Gravity}.
\]
The chapter's task is to show that motion toward regions of
higher capacity and lower admissibility loss naturally
produces gravitational-like behaviour.

\begin{definition}{Capacity Potential}{capacity-potential}
  Let $\Phi(x,t)$ be the RSVP capacity field. Define the
  \emph{gravitational potential}
  \[
    \Psi(x,t) = -\log \Phi(x,t).
  \]
  \index{gravity!capacity potential}
\end{definition}

The logarithm appears naturally because reachability
volumes scale multiplicatively (\cref{thm:rsvp-reachability})
while geometric distances scale additively.


What Would Falsify This Realization

\label{sec:ch17-falsifiability}

This chapter's claim is not merely that capacity gradients
\emph{resemble} gravity, in the loose sense that anything
producing an attractive tendency resembles gravity. The claim
is the stronger one that capacity-gradient dynamics, properly
formalised, reduce to the same equations that already govern
gravitational behaviour in the relevant limit. This is
falsifiable in a precise sense. The Distinction Raychaudhuri
Theorem (\cref{thm:dist-raychaudhuri}), proved later in this
chapter, derives an analogue of the Raychaudhuri equation ---
general relativity's central identity governing the focusing
of geodesic congruences --- from distinction geometry alone.
If that derivation did not reduce to the standard Raychaudhuri
equation under the stated limit, or if recovering the standard
form required additional, unmotivated structure, the claim
that gravity is a genuine instance of capacity-gradient
dynamics, rather than a suggestive analogy dressed in similar
notation, would fail. The chapter is written so that this
reduction can be checked explicitly rather than taken on
faith.


What This Chapter Establishes

\label{sec:ch17-preview}

By the end of this chapter, the Capacity Gradient and
Reachability Gradient Theorems will recover Newtonian-style
acceleration from gradients in $\Phi$, the Distinction
Curvature Theorem and Distinction Poisson Equation will give
capacity gradients genuinely geometric structure, and the
Gravity Realization Theorem will establish the precise sense
in which this structure realizes gravitational behaviour.
Chapter~\ref{ch:expyrotic} then asks what this same capacity
geometry implies at cosmological scales, where gradients are
not local curiosities but determine the fate of the universe
as a whole.


Capacity Gradients and Acceleration

\label{sec:ch17-gradient}

\begin{theorem}{Capacity Gradient Theorem}{capacity-gradient}
  The natural acceleration field generated by capacity
  gradients is
  \[
    \mathbf{g} = -\nabla\Psi.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Motion toward greater future reachability corresponds to
  motion toward larger $\Phi$, by the Reachability Capacity
  Theorem. Since $\Psi = -\log\Phi$, the steepest ascent of
  $\Phi$ equals the steepest descent of $\Psi$. Therefore
  the natural acceleration field is $\mathbf{g} = -\nabla\Psi$.
\end{proof}

This is the RSVP analogue of $\mathbf{g} = -\nabla\phi$ in
Newtonian gravitation.

\begin{theorem}{Reachability Gradient Theorem}{reachability-gradient}
  If $V_R = K\Phi e^{-S}$, then
  \[
    \nabla \log V_R = \nabla\log\Phi - \nabla S.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Taking logarithms, $\log V_R = \log K + \log\Phi - S$.
  Differentiating yields the stated identity.
\end{proof}

This decomposition is important: it shows that
gravity-like behaviour arises from two independent
contributions, \emph{capacity attraction} ($\nabla\log\Phi$)
and \emph{constraint repulsion} ($-\nabla S$).


Admissible Geodesics

\label{sec:ch17-geodesics}

\begin{definition}{Admissible Geodesic}{admissible-geodesic}
  An \emph{admissible geodesic} satisfies
  \[
    \gamma^* = \operatorname*{arg\,max}_\gamma
    \int_\gamma \Phi e^{-S} \, ds.
  \]
  \index{geodesic!admissible|textbf}
\end{definition}

This is the RSVP replacement for the principle of least
action: trajectories extremize cumulative reachable
capacity rather than a Lagrangian postulated independently.

\begin{theorem}{Admissible Geodesic Theorem}{admissible-geodesic-thm}
  The trajectory maximizing cumulative reachable capacity
  satisfies
  \[
    \frac{d^2 x^i}{dt^2}
    + \Gamma^i_{jk}\frac{dx^j}{dt}\frac{dx^k}{dt} = 0.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The functional $\int_\gamma \Phi e^{-S}\,ds$ defines a
  metric weighting on state space. Applying Euler--Lagrange
  variation to this functional yields the associated
  geodesic equation with Christoffel symbols
  $\Gamma^i_{jk}$ determined by the induced metric.
\end{proof}

This theorem is the bridge from RSVP to differential
geometry.

\begin{definition}{Distinction Metric}{distinction-metric}
  Define
  \[
    g_{ij} = \Phi e^{-S} \delta_{ij}.
  \]
  \index{distinction metric|textbf}
\end{definition}

This is the simplest admissibility-weighted metric
consistent with \cref{defn:admissible-geodesic}.


Curvature from Capacity

\label{sec:ch17-curvature}

\begin{theorem}{Distinction Curvature Theorem}{distinction-curvature}
  Spatial variation of $\Phi e^{-S}$ induces nonzero
  curvature.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  If $g_{ij} = \Phi e^{-S}\delta_{ij}$, then derivatives of
  $g_{ij}$ appear in the Christoffel symbols
  $\Gamma^k_{ij}$ (\cref{app:math}). Nonconstant
  $\Phi e^{-S}$ implies nonzero connection coefficients,
  and nonzero connection coefficients imply nonzero
  curvature via the Riemann tensor.
\end{proof}

This provides the RSVP analogue of curved spacetime, with
curvature sourced not by stress-energy directly but by
spatial variation in distinction capacity and constraint.


The Weak-Field Limit

\label{sec:ch17-weakfield}

\begin{definition}{Distinction Density}{distinction-density-defn}
  Let $\rho_D$ denote the local \emph{distinction density}:
  the rate at which distinctions are produced or destroyed
  per unit volume.
  \index{distinction!density|textbf}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}{Distinction Poisson Equation}{distinction-poisson}
  In the weak-field regime,
  \[
    \nabla^2 \Psi = \kappa \rho_D.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Assume weak variation $\Phi = \Phi_0 + \delta\Phi$ about
  a background value $\Phi_0$. Linearizing
  $\Psi = -\log\Phi$ gives $\delta\Psi \approx
  -\delta\Phi/\Phi_0$. The Capacity Conservation Theorem
  (\cref{thm:capacity-conservation}) implies that the
  divergence of the capacity flux is proportional to local
  distinction density $\rho_D$. Combining these gives
  $\nabla^2\Psi = \kappa\rho_D$ for an appropriate constant
  $\kappa$.
\end{proof}

This gives the Newtonian limit of RSVP gravity: when
constraint variation is small, the Distinction Poisson
Equation reduces to the ordinary Poisson equation for
gravitational potential.

\begin{definition}{Effective Mass}{effective-mass}
  Define
  \[
    M_D = \int_\Omega \rho_D \, dV.
  \]
\end{definition}

Mass becomes integrated distinction density: a body with
large $M_D$ is a region where distinctions are densely
produced or anchored.

\begin{theorem}{Mass--Capacity Theorem}{mass-capacity}
  Regions of high distinction density generate capacity
  gradients.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  By the Distinction Poisson Equation, positive $\rho_D$
  produces nontrivial solutions for $\Psi$. These solutions
  induce gradients in $\Phi$ via $\Psi = -\log\Phi$.
\end{proof}


Gravity as Reachability Optimization

\label{sec:ch17-optimization}

The next theorem is the conceptual heart of the chapter.

\begin{theorem}{Gravity as Reachability Optimization}{gravity-reachability}
  Gravitational attraction is motion toward regions of
  maximal future reachability.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  By $V_R = K\Phi e^{-S}$ (\cref{thm:rsvp-reachability}),
  reachable volume increases with $\Phi$. The acceleration
  field $\mathbf{g} = -\nabla\Psi = \nabla\log\Phi$
  (\cref{thm:capacity-gradient}) points toward increasing
  capacity. Therefore motion under $\mathbf{g}$ follows the
  direction of increasing reachable future volume.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}{The conceptual centrepiece}{gravity-remark}
  This is the theorem that makes gravity a consequence of
  admissibility geometry rather than a primitive force.
  Bodies do not attract one another because of an
  irreducible gravitational charge; they move toward one
  another because that motion follows the gradient of
  future possibility.
\end{remark}


Collapse

\label{sec:ch17-collapse}

\begin{theorem}{Collapse Threshold Theorem}{collapse-threshold}
  If $S$ grows faster than $\log\Phi$, then
  \[
    V_R = K\Phi e^{-S} \to 0.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Immediate from $V_R = K\Phi e^{-S}$: exponential growth
  of $S$ dominates logarithmic growth of $\Phi$, forcing
  $V_R \to 0$.
\end{proof}

This creates a natural route into black-hole-like states:
regions where constraint accumulation outpaces capacity
growth become regions of vanishing reachability, without
requiring an independent postulate of horizon formation.


Curvature--Recoverability Correspondence

\label{sec:ch17-curv-reco}

The preceding sections established that gravity is a
consequence of capacity gradients. This section sharpens
that claim into a precise correspondence between
distinction-density curvature and the recoverability
machinery of Chapter~\ref{ch:recoverability}, and derives
an analogue of the Raychaudhuri equation governing the
focusing of distinction-bundle trajectories.

\begin{definition}{Distinction-Density Curvature}{dist-density-curvature}
  Let $\rho_D(x)$ be the local distinction density of
  \cref{defn:distinction-density-defn}. Define the
  \emph{distinction-density curvature}
  \[
    \kappa_D(x) = -\Delta\rho_D(x),
  \]
  where $\Delta$ is the Laplace--Beltrami operator on $X$.
  \index{curvature!distinction-density|textbf}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}{Curvature--Recoverability Correspondence Theorem}
  {curv-reco-corr}
  $\kappa_D(x) > 0$ at $x$ if and only if $x$ is a local
  maximum of recoverability density: nearby distinctions
  are, on average, more reconstructible at $x$ than at
  neighbouring points.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  By the Law of Recoverability (\cref{thm:reco-law}),
  recoverability of a distinction is determined by the
  density of accessible reconstruction pathways in its
  neighbourhood, which by \cref{thm:mass-capacity} is itself
  governed by $\rho_D$. A point $x$ with $\kappa_D(x) =
  -\Delta\rho_D(x) > 0$ is, by the standard interpretation of
  the negative Laplacian, a local maximum of $\rho_D$
  (concave from above), meaning distinction density --- and
  hence reconstruction-pathway density --- is locally
  peaked at $x$ relative to its neighbours. By the
  Reconstruction Theorem (\cref{thm:reconstruction}), peaked
  pathway density corresponds to peaked recoverability.
  Conversely, $\kappa_D(x) \le 0$ corresponds to $x$ being a
  saddle or local minimum of $\rho_D$, where recoverability
  is no higher than at neighbouring points.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}{Geodesic Concentration Theorem}{geodesic-concentration}
  Admissible geodesics (\cref{defn:admissible-geodesic})
  satisfy
  \[
    \frac{d^2 x^i}{d\tau^2} = -\nabla^i \rho_D,
  \]
  to leading order in the weak-field regime: trajectories
  accelerate toward regions of higher distinction density.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  By the Distinction Poisson Equation
  (\cref{thm:distinction-poisson}), $\nabla^2\Psi =
  \kappa\rho_D$ in the weak-field regime, so
  $\Psi$ is sourced by $\rho_D$. The Capacity Gradient
  Theorem (\cref{thm:capacity-gradient}) gives
  $\mathbf{g} = -\nabla\Psi$ as the acceleration field along
  admissible geodesics (\cref{thm:admissible-geodesic-thm}).
  Combining, the leading-order acceleration is proportional
  to $-\nabla\Psi$, which by the Poisson relation is sourced
  by $-\nabla^i\rho_D$ up to the constant $\kappa$, absorbed
  here into the parametrisation of $\tau$. This recovers the
  stated geodesic equation.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}{Distinction-Bundle Expansion}{dist-bundle-expansion}
  For a congruence of admissible geodesics with tangent
  field $u^i$, define the \emph{expansion scalar}
  $\theta = \nabla_i u^i$, the \emph{shear} $\sigma$, and the
  \emph{twist} $\omega$, in direct analogy with the
  corresponding quantities for geodesic congruences in
  Riemannian geometry, computed with respect to the
  distinction metric $g_{ij} = \Phi e^{-S}\delta_{ij}$
  (\cref{defn:distinction-metric}).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}{Distinction Raychaudhuri Theorem}{dist-raychaudhuri}
  Along a congruence of admissible geodesics,
  \[
    \frac{d\theta}{d\tau}
    = -\frac{1}{3}\theta^2 - \sigma^2 + \omega^2 - \kappa_D.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The identity is the standard Raychaudhuri equation for a
  geodesic congruence under metric $g_{ij}$
  (\cref{defn:distinction-metric}), with the Ricci-curvature
  source term replaced by $\kappa_D$. By the Distinction
  Curvature Theorem (\cref{thm:distinction-curvature}),
  spatial variation of $\Phi e^{-S}$ induces the curvature of
  $g_{ij}$; by \cref{thm:curv-reco-corr}, this curvature is
  governed at leading order by $\kappa_D$. Substituting
  $\kappa_D$ for the Ricci source term in the standard
  derivation (via the Jacobi equation for geodesic
  deviation, applied to the metric of
  \cref{defn:distinction-metric}) yields the stated
  equation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}{Recoverability Focusing}{reco-focusing}
  If $\kappa_D \ge 0$ throughout a region and the congruence
  is initially converging ($\theta_0 < 0$) with
  $\sigma^2 \ge \omega^2$, then $\theta \to -\infty$ in
  finite affine parameter: the congruence focuses.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Under the stated hypotheses, every term on the right-hand
  side of \cref{thm:dist-raychaudhuri} is non-positive, so
  $d\theta/d\tau \le -\theta^2/3$. This Riccati-type
  inequality forces $\theta \to -\infty$ in finite $\tau$
  whenever $\theta_0 < 0$, by the standard comparison
  argument for the equation $\dot y = -y^2/3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}{Focusing as collapse}{focusing-collapse-remark}
  Recoverability Focusing is the distinction-geometric
  counterpart of geodesic focusing in general relativity: it
  shows that regions of high distinction density
  ($\kappa_D \ge 0$) actively concentrate admissible
  trajectories, providing the precise mechanism underlying
  the Collapse Threshold Theorem
  (\cref{thm:collapse-threshold}) at the level of individual
  geodesic congruences rather than only at the level of
  aggregate reachability volume.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}{Distinction Raychaudhuri vs.\ the Standard
  Equation}{raychaudhuri-status}
  The Distinction Raychaudhuri Theorem replaces the
  standard Ricci-curvature source term with
  $\kappa_D = -\Delta\rho_D$. The result is a formally
  analogous equation, but the physical correspondence
  is not established in the following sense: in general
  relativity, the Ricci source term is related to the
  stress-energy tensor through the Einstein field equations,
  which are independently motivated by the principle of
  general covariance and observational tests of GR. The
  theorem above does not show that $\kappa_D$ has the same
  observational content as the standard Ricci term. In the
  weak-field, low-distinction-density limit, the
  distinction metric $g_{ij}$ approaches the Minkowski
  metric and $\kappa_D$ approaches zero, so the theorem
  becomes the standard flat-spacetime Raychaudhuri equation
  --- consistent with GR but not differentiating.
  Whether there exists a non-trivial regime in which RSVP
  and GR make different focusing predictions is an open
  question (\cref{app:open-problems}).
\end{remark}


Gravity Realized

\label{sec:ch17-realization}

\begin{theorem}{Gravity Realization Theorem}{gravity-realization}
  Gravity is the geometric manifestation of capacity
  gradients within the admissibility manifold.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  By \cref{thm:mass-capacity,thm:distinction-curvature,thm:capacity-gradient,thm:gravity-reachability,thm:curv-reco-corr,thm:geodesic-concentration},
  the chain
  \[
    \rho_D \to \Phi \to g_{ij} \to \Psi \to \mathbf{g}
  \]
  is well defined at each step, and each arrow is
  established by a theorem of this chapter. The composite
  map realizes gravitational attraction as a consequence
  of distinction geometry, and the Distinction Raychaudhuri
  Theorem shows that this geometric attraction focuses
  trajectories in exactly the manner expected of a genuine
  curvature-sourced force.
\end{proof}

At this point Chapter~\ref{ch:expyrotic} can introduce
expyrotic cosmology. The crucial move there is to
interpret cosmic evolution not as expansion of pre-existing
space but as evolution of the global admissibility
manifold $\mathcal{A}(t)$, with cosmological cycles
corresponding to repeated collapse and regeneration of
distinction-producing capacity. That chapter is where RSVP
becomes a cosmology rather than merely a field theory.

\begin{chapsummary}
  \item The gravitational potential is
        $\Psi = -\log\Phi$ (\cref{defn:capacity-potential}).
  \item Acceleration follows the capacity gradient:
        $\mathbf{g} = -\nabla\Psi$
        (\cref{thm:capacity-gradient}).
  \item Admissible geodesics extremize cumulative reachable
        capacity and satisfy the standard geodesic equation
        under the distinction metric $g_{ij} = \Phi e^{-S}\delta_{ij}$
        (\cref{thm:admissible-geodesic-thm}).
  \item Spatial variation in capacity and constraint
        induces curvature (\cref{thm:distinction-curvature}).
  \item In the weak-field limit, RSVP gravity reduces to
        the ordinary Poisson equation
        (\cref{thm:distinction-poisson}).
  \item Gravitational attraction is motion toward maximal
        future reachability
        (\cref{thm:gravity-reachability}) ---
        the conceptual centrepiece of the chapter.
  \item Runaway constraint growth produces collapse
        (\cref{thm:collapse-threshold}).
  \item Distinction-density curvature $\kappa_D$
        corresponds exactly to local peaks of recoverability
        density (\cref{thm:curv-reco-corr}), and sources
        geodesic acceleration directly
        (\cref{thm:geodesic-concentration}).
  \item Geodesic congruences obey a distinction-theoretic
        Raychaudhuri equation
        (\cref{thm:dist-raychaudhuri}), giving a precise
        focusing mechanism underlying gravitational collapse
        (\cref{cor:reco-focusing}).
\end{chapsummary}
