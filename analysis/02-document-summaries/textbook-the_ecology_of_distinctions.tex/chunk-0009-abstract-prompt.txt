Reachability Replaces Size

\label{sec:ch18-replaces}

Chapter~18 is where the framework stops looking like a
field theory and becomes a cosmology. The central idea is
not ordinary expansion. The preceding chapters have already
defined the primary quantity governing a system's future:
admissible volume. The question ``how large is the
universe?'' is replaced by the question ``how much future
remains reachable?'' In this formulation, cosmology becomes
the dynamics of admissible volume.

Chapter~\ref{ch:gravity} derived gravity from capacity
gradients via the Gravity Realization Theorem
(\cref{thm:gravity-realization}), recovering local
gravitational behaviour without postulating mass-energy as an
independent source of curvature. This chapter extends that
derivation to the largest scale available: not the behaviour
of a single gradient, but the trajectory of an entire
universe's admissible volume over cosmological time.

Standard cosmology faces a well-known difficulty at exactly
this scale. The horizon problem asks why causally
disconnected regions of the observable universe exhibit
nearly identical temperatures, when standard Big Bang
expansion gives them no opportunity to have exchanged
information. Inflationary cosmology's solution posits a
period of exponential expansion early in the universe's
history, large enough to bring those regions within causal
contact before inflation began \parencite{guth1981}. This
solution works, in the sense that it reproduces observed
homogeneity, but it does so by positing a specific, otherwise
unmotivated inflaton field and expansion rate, calibrated
after the fact to produce the observed result. An alternative
tradition, ekpyrotic and cyclic cosmology, replaces inflation
with a contracting phase preceding the hot Big Bang, smoothing
inhomogeneities through contraction rather than expansion
\parencite{steinhardt2002}. This chapter's framework is closer
in spirit to the second tradition than the first --- both
treat homogenisation as something a prior dynamical phase
achieves rather than something imposed by fiat --- but it
departs from both by asking the question in terms of
admissible volume rather than physical scale factor. The
question this chapter asks is not how large the universe is,
or how fast it expanded, but how much admissible future
$\mathcal{V}(t)$ a given cosmological history preserves, and
what happens to $\mathcal{V}(t)$ when that history reaches its
apparent end.


What Would Falsify This Realization

\label{sec:ch18-falsifiability}

This chapter's central construction is the reintegration
operator, formalised later in the chapter as a mechanism by
which a universe approaching admissibility collapse ---
$\mathcal{V}(t)\to0$ --- can be carried through that collapse
into a renewed phase rather than terminating. The claim that
cosmological renewal is a genuine instance of repair, in the
sense of Chapter~\ref{ch:repair}, rather than an independently
postulated cosmological mechanism dressed in repair
vocabulary, is falsifiable against a specific structural test.
The Reintegration Fixed-Point Theorem
(\cref{thm:reintegration-fixed-point}), proved later in this
chapter, should be structurally identical to the Repair
Existence Theorem (\cref{thm:repair-exist}) once restricted to
the cosmological domain --- the same existence argument,
applied to a different distinction space. If the reintegration
construction required machinery not already present in the
general theory of repair, or if its fixed point failed to
correspond to the repair-theoretic fixed point under the
appropriate restriction, the claim that cosmological renewal
realizes repair, rather than merely resembling it, would fail.


What This Chapter Establishes

\label{sec:ch18-preview}

By the end of this chapter, cosmological admissible volume
will replace the scale factor as the fundamental quantity
governing a universe's history, the Admissibility Collapse
Theorem will characterise the conditions under which
$\mathcal{V}(t)\to0$, and the reintegration operator will show
how such a collapse can be survived rather than simply
endured. Chapter~\ref{ch:cosmo-completion} then asks what
happens after arbitrarily many such cycles: whether a universe
undergoing repeated reintegration approaches a final,
qualitatively different endpoint, or repeats indefinitely
without ever genuinely completing.


Reachability and Cosmological Admissible Volume

\label{sec:ch18-replaces-defs}

\begin{definition}{Cosmological Admissible Volume}{cosmo-adm}
  Define
  \[
    \mathcal{V}(t) = \Vol\bigl(\mathcal{A}(t)\bigr).
  \]
  \index{cosmology!admissible volume|textbf}
  \index{admissibility!physical}
\end{definition}

Unlike standard cosmology, the scale factor $a(t)$ is no
longer fundamental. Instead $\mathcal{V}(t)$ is fundamental.

\begin{theorem}{Cosmological Reachability Theorem}{cosmo-reach}
  The long-term future of a universe is determined by
  $\mathcal{V}(t)$ rather than by physical size.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Physical volume measures spatial extent. Reachability
  volume measures future possibility. A large universe may
  possess $\mathcal{V} \approx 0$ if all trajectories
  terminate. A small universe may possess large
  $\mathcal{V}$ if many futures remain reachable. Therefore
  future structure depends on $\mathcal{V}$ rather than on
  spatial size.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}{From geometry to possibility}{cosmo-philosophy}
  This theorem is philosophically significant: it shifts
  cosmology from a science of geometry to a science of
  possibility.
\end{remark}


Cosmological Constraint and Collapse

\label{sec:ch18-collapse}

\begin{definition}{Cosmological Constraint Functional}{cosmo-constraint}
  \[
    \mathcal{S}(t) = \int_U S(x,t)\, dV.
  \]
\end{definition}

This is the universe-wide RSVP entropy field, integrated
over the entire spatial domain $U$.

\begin{theorem}{Admissibility Collapse Theorem}{adm-collapse}
  If $\mathcal{S}(t) \to \infty$ while $\Phi$ remains
  bounded, then $\mathcal{V}(t) \to
  0$.\index{collapse!admissibility collapse|textbf}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  By Chapter~16, $V_R = K\Phi e^{-S}$. Integrating over the
  universe gives
  \[
    \mathcal{V} = K \int_U \Phi e^{-S} \, dV.
  \]
  As $S \to \infty$ pointwise with $\Phi$ bounded, the
  exponential factor vanishes throughout $U$. Therefore
  $\mathcal{V} \to 0$.
\end{proof}

This is the RSVP analogue of heat death.

\begin{definition}{Admissibility Singularity}{adm-singularity}
  An \emph{admissibility singularity} occurs when
  $\mathcal{V}(t) = 0$.
  \index{singularity!admissibility|textbf}
\end{definition}

Notice that no divergence of density and no divergence of
curvature is required for this singularity. It is defined
purely by exhaustion of futures.


Expyrotic Necessity

\label{sec:ch18-necessity}

\begin{theorem}{Expyrotic Necessity Theorem}{expyrotic-necessity}
  A universe satisfying $\mathcal{V}(t) \to 0$ must either
  terminate or undergo distinction renewal.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  When $\mathcal{V} = 0$, no future trajectories remain, and
  continuation is impossible. To avoid termination, new
  reachable states must be generated, which requires
  creation of new distinction capacity. Therefore renewal
  is necessary if termination is to be avoided.
\end{proof}

This theorem is essentially the Principle of Regeneration
(Chapter~\ref{ch:regeneration}) applied
cosmologically.

\begin{definition}{Cosmological Renewal Operator}{cosmo-renewal}
  \[
    \mathfrak{E} : \mathcal{A}_{\min} \to \mathcal{A}_{\max}.
  \]
  \index{expyrotic renewal operator|textbf}
  \index{regeneration!cosmological|textbf}
\end{definition}

The expyrotic operator $\mathfrak{E}$ maps near-zero
admissibility volume to renewed admissibility volume.

\begin{theorem}{Expyrotic Cycle Theorem}{expyrotic-cycle}
  A cosmological cycle satisfies
  \[
    \mathcal{V}_{\max} \to \mathcal{V}_{\min}
    \xrightarrow{\ \mathfrak{E}\ } \mathcal{V}_{\max}.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Entropy accumulation reduces admissible volume. By the
  Admissibility Collapse Theorem, $\mathcal{V}$ approaches
  zero. The renewal operator $\mathfrak{E}$ creates new
  distinction capacity, by definition. Therefore
  $\mathcal{V}$ returns to a high value after renewal.
\end{proof}


Cosmological Evolution

\label{sec:ch18-evolution}

\begin{definition}{Cosmological Potential}{cosmo-potential}
  \[
    \mathcal{U}(\mathcal{V}) = \lambda \mathcal{S} - \mu \Phi.
  \]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}{Cosmological Evolution Equation}{cosmo-evolution}
  The admissibility volume obeys
  \[
    \frac{d\mathcal{V}}{dt} = \alpha \Phi - \beta \mathcal{S}.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Capacity production increases reachable futures at rate
  proportional to $\Phi$; constraint accumulation decreases
  reachable futures at rate proportional to $\mathcal{S}$.
  Combining both contributions linearly yields
  $\frac{d\mathcal{V}}{dt} = \alpha\Phi - \beta\mathcal{S}$.
\end{proof}

This is the simplest cosmological RSVP equation consistent
with the Capacity Conservation and Constraint Accumulation
Theorems of Chapter~16.

\begin{theorem}{Apparent Expansion Theorem}{apparent-expansion}
  Growth of $\mathcal{V}(t)$ can be observed as spatial
  expansion even when the primary dynamics occur in
  admissibility space.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Increasing admissible volume enlarges the set of
  reachable configurations. Observers embedded within the
  manifold interpret this enlargement as increasing spatial
  separation between distinguishable configurations, since
  spatial separation is itself a derived distinction.
  Hence admissibility growth may project as cosmological
  expansion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}{Expansion as interpretive equivalence}{expansion-projection}
  The theorem establishes that a distinction-theoretic
  description can reproduce the phenomenology associated
  with cosmological expansion: observers embedded in the
  manifold register growth in admissible volume as
  increasing spatial separation between distinguishable
  configurations. It does not establish that physical
  metric expansion is absent, or that standard cosmological
  models are wrong. The theorem therefore provides an
  \emph{alternative interpretive framework} that is
  consistent with observed expansion, not a falsification
  of the standard account. Whether the two frameworks
  diverge in any empirically accessible regime requires
  the derivations that, as the following remark notes,
  the theorem does not yet supply.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}{Status of the Apparent Expansion Theorem}
  {apparent-expansion-status}
  The proof above is an interpretive argument, not a
  physical derivation. It does not supply:
  \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
    \item a correspondence between the admissibility
          metric $g_{ij} = \Phi e^{-S}\delta_{ij}$ and
          the FLRW metric of standard cosmology;
    \item a derivation of the redshift-magnitude relation
          from $\mathcal{V}(t)$ growth; or
    \item a prediction distinguishing RSVP cosmological
          expansion from standard $\Lambda$CDM in any
          presently accessible observational regime.
  \end{enumerate}
  What the theorem does establish is a conceptual
  reframing: \emph{if} spatial separation is a derived
  distinction (as argued in Chapter~\ref{ch:geometry-futures}),
  and \emph{if} admissible volume grows, \emph{then} what
  appears as metric expansion can be reread as growth in
  the set of distinguishable configurations. This is a
  philosophical interpretation of cosmological expansion,
  not an independent physical derivation of it. Whether
  the interpretation can be made precise enough to make
  distinguishable predictions is an open question listed
  in \cref{app:open-problems}.
\end{remark}


Cosmological Memory

\label{sec:ch18-memory}

\begin{theorem}{Cosmological Memory Theorem}{cosmo-memory}
  Successive expyrotic cycles need not be independent.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  If $\reco(\mathcal{U}) > 0$, then information from
  previous cycles survives renewal. By the Law of
  Recoverability (Chapter~\ref{ch:recoverability}), partial
  reconstruction remains possible across the renewal
  operator $\mathfrak{E}$. Therefore cosmological memory may
  persist.
\end{proof}

This is the direct cosmological analogue of repair, and it
sets up Chapter~19's treatment of cross-cycle conservation.


Expyrotic Admissibility

\label{sec:ch18-admissibility}

\begin{theorem}{Expyrotic Admissibility Theorem}{expyrotic-adm}
  Cosmological renewal is generatively admissible.
  \parencite{steinhardt2002,penrose2010}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Renewal transforms $\mathcal{V}_{\min}$ into
  $\mathcal{V}_{\max}$, so $\Delta\mathcal{V} > 0$. By the
  Generative Admissibility Principle
  (\cref{prc:gap-final}), $\frac{d}{dt}\Vol(\mathcal{A}) \ge 0$
  defines generative admissibility. Hence cosmological
  renewal satisfies the defining condition of generative
  admissibility.
\end{proof}

This theorem is the reason Chapter~15 already cited
expyrotic renewal as a corollary of the Generative
Admissibility Principle. It closes the loop between
cosmology and the rest of the book: the universe itself
becomes the largest regenerative system, cycling between
admissibility exhaustion and admissibility renewal. In the
architecture of this monograph, the Big Bang is no longer
the beginning of existence but one instance of a more
general repair operation acting on the global distinction
manifold.


The Reintegration Operator

\label{sec:ch18-reintegration}

The Expyrotic Cycle Theorem (\cref{thm:expyrotic-cycle})
asserts the existence of a renewal operator
$\mathfrak{E}:\mathcal{A}_{\min}\to\mathcal{A}_{\max}$
without specifying its mechanism. This section supplies a
concrete construction --- a reintegration operator acting
on cosmic microwave background structure --- and proves that
it possesses a fixed point characterising the
post-renewal state, together with a theorem on the
asymptotic suppression of large-scale entropy gradients.

\begin{definition}{Reintegration Kernel}{reintegration-kernel}
  Let $\Phi_\mathrm{CMB}(x,t)$ denote the capacity field
  restricted to the cosmic microwave background surface, and
  let $K:\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\ge0}$ be an
  integrable kernel supported on $[0,T_P]$ for renewal
  period $T_P$. The \emph{reintegration operator} is
  \[
    (\mathcal{K}\Phi)(x,t)
    = \int_{t-T_P}^{t} K(t-t')\, \Phi_\mathrm{CMB}(x,t')\, dt'.
  \]
  \index{reintegration operator|textbf}
\end{definition}

The reintegration operator aggregates capacity-field
structure from the preceding cycle, weighted by recency
through $K$, to construct the capacity field with which the
next cycle begins.

\begin{theorem}{Reintegration Fixed-Point Theorem}{reintegration-fixed-point}
  Suppose $\mathcal{K}$ acts on a compact, convex set of
  admissible capacity-field configurations
  $\mathcal{C} \subset \{\Phi_\mathrm{CMB}\}$ and is
  continuous on $\mathcal{C}$. Then there exists
  $\Phi^*\in\mathcal{C}$ with $\Phi^* = \mathcal{K}(\Phi^*)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  By construction, $\mathcal{K}$ is a bounded linear
  (hence continuous) operator on the space of capacity-field
  configurations, since it is given by convolution against
  the integrable kernel $K$. Under the stated compactness
  hypothesis on $\mathcal{C}$ and convexity, the
  Schauder Fixed-Point Theorem applies directly to
  $\mathcal{K}|_\mathcal{C} : \mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}$
  (well-defined since $\mathcal{K}$ preserves admissibility
  by the non-negativity of $K$ and $\Phi_\mathrm{CMB}$),
  giving existence of $\Phi^*\in\mathcal{C}$ with
  $\Phi^* = \mathcal{K}(\Phi^*)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}{Interpretation of the fixed point}{reintegration-interpretation}
  The fixed point $\Phi^*$ represents a self-consistent
  post-renewal capacity configuration: a capacity field
  that, when reintegrated through $\mathcal{K}$ across one
  renewal period, reproduces itself. This is the
  mathematical content of the Cosmological Memory Theorem
  (\cref{thm:cosmo-memory}): it identifies the specific
  configurations through which cross-cycle correlation
  $\reco(\mathcal{U})>0$ can be realized, rather than merely
  asserting their possible existence.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}{Asymptotic Entropy-Gradient Suppression Theorem}
  {entropy-gradient-suppression}
  Under reintegration via a fixed point $\Phi^*$ of
  \cref{thm:reintegration-fixed-point}, large-scale entropy
  gradients are asymptotically suppressed:
  \[
    \lim_{t\to\infty} \nabla S(x,t) = 0
  \]
  uniformly on length scales exceeding the reintegration
  correlation length $\ell_K$ determined by the support of
  $K$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  By the Constraint Accumulation Theorem
  (\cref{thm:constraint-accumulation-rsvp}),
  $\partial S/\partial t = \alpha D - \beta R + \gamma\nabla^2 S$.
  At the fixed point $\Phi^*$, reintegration injects capacity
  uniformly on scales up to $\ell_K$ (by the averaging
  property of convolution against $K$), which by the
  Lamphrodyne Stability Theorem (\cref{thm:lamphrodyne})
  drives $\partial S/\partial\Phi \to 0$ on those scales as
  $t\to\infty$, since the system relaxes toward the fixed
  point along trajectories satisfying
  $dS/dt = -\mu(\partial S/\partial\Phi)^2 \le 0$ with
  equality only when $\partial S/\partial\Phi = 0$. Spatial
  uniformity of the injected capacity on scales $\ge\ell_K$
  forces $S$ itself to become spatially uniform on those
  scales in the limit, hence $\nabla S \to 0$ uniformly
  there.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}{Horizon smoothing}{horizon-smoothing-remark}
  The Asymptotic Entropy-Gradient Suppression Theorem
  supplies the mathematical mechanism behind horizon
  smoothing in the expyrotic picture: reintegration through
  $\mathcal{K}$ does not merely renew admissible volume in
  aggregate (\cref{thm:expyrotic-adm}) but actively erases
  large-scale entropy gradients left over from the
  preceding cycle, in direct analogy with the role played by
  inflation in suppressing large-scale inhomogeneity in the
  standard cosmological picture --- but derived here from
  the reintegration operator rather than postulated as an
  independent inflationary phase.
\end{remark}


On the Speculative Status of This Chapter

\label{sec:ch18-speculative-status}

The theorems in this chapter are genuine mathematical
results within the RSVP framework. The Expyrotic Necessity
Theorem, the Reintegration Operator, and the Cosmological
Memory Theorem follow from the formal apparatus of
Chapters~\ref{ch:rsvp}--\ref{ch:gravity}. What they do
not do, and do not claim to do, is derive cosmological
dynamics from physical first principles.

Specifically, the chapter does not show:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item that the reintegration operator $\mathfrak{E}$ is
        consistent with energy-momentum conservation or
        with the laws of known cosmological dynamics;
  \item that the expyrotic transition predicts any
        observation distinguishable from standard
        inflationary or cyclic cosmological models; or
  \item that $\mathcal{V}(t)$ as defined here is
        measurable, or that its approach to zero
        corresponds to a specific observational signature
        in the cosmic microwave background, large-scale
        structure, or gravitational wave spectrum.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}{Reading the Cosmological Chapters}
  {cosmo-reading-guide}
  The expyrotic cosmology and cosmological completion
  chapters establish a structural result: \emph{any} physical
  system that obeys the RSVP field equations and approaches
  an admissibility singularity must satisfy the conditions
  the Expyrotic Necessity Theorem identifies. This is a
  theorem about the RSVP framework, not an independent
  derivation of cyclic cosmology from observational data.
  The connection to Steinhardt--Turok cyclic models, Penrose
  conformal cyclic cosmology, and related proposals is one
  of \emph{structural consonance} --- the formal patterns
  rhyme --- rather than derivation. Whether the RSVP
  framework constitutes a genuine physical proposal, and
  what observations would test it, is explicitly listed as
  an open problem in \cref{app:open-problems}.
\end{remark}

These caveats do not diminish the content of the chapter.
They place it accurately. Structural necessity results ---
theorems that show what \emph{must} hold within a formal
system --- have a long and legitimate history in
theoretical physics, from thermodynamics before statistical
mechanics, to the Penrose singularity theorems before
quantum gravity, to the holographic bounds before string
theory. This chapter belongs in that tradition: a
structural result whose physical interpretation is
established but whose empirical derivation remains future
work.

What Is Conserved

\label{sec:ch19-conserved}

Chapter~19 introduces no fundamentally new machinery. Its
role is to complete the cosmology of Chapter~18 and connect
the physical realization back to the rest of the monograph.
Chapters~16--18 introduced the RSVP triple
$(\Phi,\mathbf{v},S)$, gravity as admissibility geometry,
and expyrotic renewal. The remaining question is: what
global quantity is actually conserved across cycles? The
answer is not matter, not energy, and not information in
the Shannon sense. The natural conserved object in this
framework is distinction-producing capacity.

The traditional alternative endpoint for a cosmological
history is heat death: the prediction, following directly
from an unbounded Second Law, that entropy increases without
limit until the universe reaches a state of maximum entropy
and uniform temperature, after which no further structure can
be extracted or sustained \parencite{prigogine1984}. Heat
death is not wrong as a statement about entropy; it follows
from the Second Law as standardly formulated. What it lacks
is any mechanism, internal to the standard account, by which a
universe approaching maximum entropy could do anything other
than approach it asymptotically and remain there. The Second
Law of Distinction Dynamics (\cref{law:second-law-dist})
already re-derived entropy increase as distinction erosion in
the absence of repair; this chapter asks what changes once
repair --- in its cosmological form, reintegration --- is
taken into account at the scale of the universe as a whole,
and whether the resulting endpoint is heat death after all, or
something importantly different.


What Would Falsify This Realization

\label{sec:ch19-falsifiability}

The claim this chapter defends is that ``saturation without
exhaustion'' and ``heat death'' are not two names for the same
endpoint described with different vocabulary, but genuinely
distinguishable predictions on the same field content. This is
the falsifiable core of the chapter. The Asymptotic Saturation
Theorem (\cref{thm:asymptotic-saturation}), proved later in
this chapter, should be directly contrastable with a
heat-death model built on the identical RSVP field content
introduced in Chapter~\ref{ch:rsvp}: the two models share
$(\Phi,\mathbf{v},S)$ but diverge in their long-run prediction
for $\mathcal{C}(t)$. If the asymptotic-saturation construction
collapsed onto the heat-death prediction once worked through in
detail --- if reintegration turned out to conserve nothing
that a standard thermodynamic treatment does not already
conserve --- the claim that this framework predicts something
genuinely different from heat death, rather than relabelling
it, would fail. The No Terminal Equilibrium Theorem is written
to make this divergence explicit and checkable against the
standard model's own asymptotic behaviour.


What This Chapter Establishes

\label{sec:ch19-preview}

By the end of this chapter, the Cosmological Capacity

\begin{definition}{Cosmological Distinction Capacity}{cosmo-distinction-capacity}
  Define
  \[
    \mathcal{C}(t) = \int_U \Phi(x,t)\, e^{-S(x,t)} \, dV.
  \]
\end{definition}

This is the global admissible capacity of the universe. It
is closely related to $\mathcal{V}(t)$ but distinct: $\mathcal{V}$
measures actual admissible volume, while $\mathcal{C}$
measures the capacity to generate admissible volume.


Cosmological Capacity Conservation

\label{sec:ch19-conservation}

\begin{theorem}{Cosmological Capacity Conservation}{cosmo-capacity}
  For a closed RSVP universe,
  \[
    \frac{d}{dt}\bigl(\mathcal{C} + \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{hidden}}\bigr)
    = 0,
  \]
  where $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{hidden}}$ represents
  distinction capacity inaccessible to current observers.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Local entropy production reduces observable $\Phi e^{-S}$.
  However, Chapter~\ref{ch:recoverability} established that
  destruction and dispersal are not equivalent: lost
  capacity may become inaccessible without being
  annihilated. Therefore observable capacity decreases
  while hidden capacity increases by the same amount, and
  the sum remains constant.
\end{proof}

This is the cosmological version of recoverability,
extending the Law of Recoverability (\cref{thm:reco-law})
to the scale of the universe as a whole.

\begin{definition}{Distinction Horizon}{dist-horizon}
  The \emph{distinction horizon} is
  \[
    \mathcal{H}_D = \partial \mathcal{A}.
  \]
  \index{horizon!distinction|textbf}
\end{definition}

This replaces the ordinary notion of a cosmological or
event horizon with a boundary defined purely in terms of
admissibility.

\begin{theorem}{Distinction Horizon Theorem}{dist-horizon-thm}
  A region beyond $\mathcal{H}_D$ is unreachable but not
  necessarily nonexistent.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The horizon $\mathcal{H}_D$ is defined by loss of
  admissible trajectories, not by loss of ontological
  status. Existence is not equivalent to reachability
  (\cref{ch:reachability}). Therefore crossing $\mathcal{H}_D$
  removes accessibility rather than removing existence.
\end{proof}

This theorem links directly to the Future Cone Theorem of
Chapter~\ref{ch:reachability}.


Memory Across Cycles

\label{sec:ch19-memory}

\begin{definition}{Cycle Recoverability}{cycle-recoverability}
  \[
    \reco_c = \frac{I_{\mathrm{recovered}}}{I_{\mathrm{previous}}}.
  \]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}{Cycle Memory Theorem}{cycle-memory}
  If $\reco_c > 0$, successive cosmological cycles are
  correlated.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Positive recoverability implies partial reconstruction
  of prior-cycle distinctions, by the Reconstruction
  Theorem (\cref{thm:reconstruction}). Recovered
  distinctions constrain future evolution, since they
  enter as boundary conditions on the renewal operator
  $\mathfrak{E}$. Therefore cycles are not statistically
  independent.
\end{proof}

This is the cosmological analogue of memory developed in
Chapter~\ref{ch:memory}.

\begin{theorem}{Universe as Repair System}{universe-repair}
  Expyrotic renewal is a repair operator acting on the
  global distinction
  manifold.\index{regeneration!cosmological}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Let $\mathcal{A}_{\min}$ denote the collapsed
  admissibility manifold. Renewal maps
  $\mathcal{A}_{\min} \to \mathcal{A}_{\max}$, restoring
  future distinction-production capacity. By the Repair
  Operator definition (\cref{defn:repair-op}) and the Repair
  Existence Theorem (\cref{thm:repair-exist}), this map
  satisfies the defining conditions of repair.
\end{proof}


No Terminal Equilibrium

\label{sec:ch19-no-terminal}

\begin{theorem}{No Terminal Equilibrium Theorem}{no-terminal-equilibrium}
  If renewal remains possible, $\mathcal{V} = 0$ cannot be
  a permanent state.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Permanent heat death requires $\mathcal{V}(t) = 0$ for all
  future $t$. By the Expyrotic Necessity Theorem
  (\cref{thm:expyrotic-necessity}), if renewal remains
  possible, then there exists $t^*$ such that
  $\mathcal{V}(t^*) > 0$, contradicting permanence.
\end{proof}


Cosmological Productivity

\label{sec:ch19-productivity}

\begin{definition}{Cosmological Productivity}{cosmo-productivity}
  \[
    P_U(t) = \frac{dD_U}{dt},
  \]
  where $D_U$ is total distinction capacity of the
  universe.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}{Cosmological Productivity Theorem}{cosmo-productivity-thm}
  A cosmological phase is generative iff $P_U(t) > 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Positive productivity creates new distinction structures.
  By the Future Distinction Dominance Theorem
  (Chapter~\ref{ch:preservation}), new distinctions
  increase future admissible volume. Therefore positive
  $P_U$ is equivalent to a generative phase, and conversely
  a generative phase requires net distinction creation,
  i.e.\ $P_U(t) > 0$.
\end{proof}


Cosmological Optimality

\label{sec:ch19-optimality}

\begin{theorem}{Cosmological Optimality Theorem}{cosmo-optimality}
  Among cosmological trajectories satisfying the same
  conservation laws, generatively admissible trajectories
  maximize asymptotic distinction capacity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  By the Future Distinction Optimality Theorem
  (\cref{thm:gat-final}), $\frac{d}{dt}\Vol(\mathcal{A}) \ge 0$
  dominates extractive alternatives over a sufficiently
  long horizon. Applying this theorem to the universe as a
  whole, with $\mathcal{A}$ interpreted as the global
  admissibility manifold, gives maximal asymptotic $D_U$
  among trajectories sharing the same conserved quantities.
\end{proof}

This theorem imports the entire admissibility programme of
Part~V into cosmology without modification.


Asymptotic Saturation Without Exhaustion

\label{sec:ch19-saturation}

The chapter's results so far establish conservation
(\cref{thm:cosmo-capacity}), boundedness of horizons
(\cref{thm:dist-horizon-thm}), and optimality among
generative trajectories (\cref{thm:cosmo-optimality}). What
remains is to state precisely the asymptotic regime toward
which a generatively admissible universe tends: local
distinction production saturates, while global admissible
volume remains permanently bounded away from zero.

\begin{definition}{Total Distinguishable Cosmological Volume}{total-dist-vol}
  Let $\mathcal{D}_t$ denote the set of distinctions
  actively produced by the universe up to cosmic time $t$.
  Define
  \[
    V_D(t) = \Vol(\mathcal{D}_t).
  \]
\end{definition}

$V_D(t)$ differs from the cosmological admissible volume
$\mathcal{V}(t)$ of \cref{defn:cosmo-adm}: $V_D$
measures\index{admissibility!physical}
distinctions actually realised, while $\mathcal{V}$ measures
the volume of futures still admissible. The Cosmological
Productivity Theorem (\cref{thm:cosmo-productivity-thm})
concerns the rate of change of total distinction capacity
$D_U$; $V_D$ is its realised, cumulative counterpart.

\begin{theorem}{Asymptotic Saturation Theorem}{asymptotic-saturation}
  Under generatively admissible cosmological dynamics with
  bounded local capacity production rate, the universe
  satisfies
  \[
    \lim_{t\to\infty} \frac{dV_D}{dt} = 0
    \qquad\text{while simultaneously}\qquad
    \limsup_{t\to\infty} \Vol(\mathcal{A}_t) > 0.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \emph{Local saturation.} By the Reintegration Fixed-Point
  Theorem (\cref{thm:reintegration-fixed-point}), the
  capacity field approaches a fixed configuration $\Phi^*$
  under repeated reintegration. At a fixed point, local
  production of new distinctions --- which by
  \cref{defn:cosmo-productivity} occurs at rate
  $P_U(t) = dD_U/dt$ --- is itself driven by the residual
  $\Phi - \Phi^*$, which tends to zero by definition of the
  fixed point. Since $V_D(t)$ accumulates realised
  distinctions at a rate bounded above by $P_U(t)$ (not
  every increment in capacity is realised as an actual
  distinction), and $P_U(t)\to0$, we obtain
  $dV_D/dt \to 0$.

  \emph{Global non-exhaustion.} By the No Terminal
  Equilibrium Theorem (\cref{thm:no-terminal-equilibrium}),
  $\mathcal{V}(t) = \Vol(\mathcal{A}_t) = 0$ cannot hold for
  all sufficiently large $t$ while renewal remains possible.
  By the Expyrotic Cycle Theorem
  (\cref{thm:expyrotic-cycle}), renewal recurs, driving
  $\mathcal{V}$ back to $\mathcal{V}_{\max} > 0$ infinitely
  often. Hence
  $\limsup_{t\to\infty}\Vol(\mathcal{A}_t) \ge
  \mathcal{V}_{\max} > 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}{Saturation is not exhaustion}{saturation-not-exhaustion}
  The Asymptotic Saturation Theorem formalises the central
  cosmological claim of this chapter: a generatively
  admissible universe need not produce unboundedly many new
  local distinctions forever to remain viable. Local
  distinction production can saturate --- $dV_D/dt\to0$ ---
  without admissible future volume ever being exhausted, so
  long as the global cycling guaranteed by the Expyrotic
  Cycle Theorem continues to refresh $\mathcal{V}(t)$ away
  from zero. Saturation of realised distinction is therefore
  compatible with, and indeed expected under, permanent
  preservation of distinction-producing \emph{capacity} ---
  exactly the distinction the Ecology of Distinctions
  Theorem (\cref{thm:ecology-final-31}) draws between
  $D_U$ and $\liminf\Vol(\adm(t))$.
\end{remark}


Completion

\label{sec:ch19-completion}

\begin{theorem}{Cosmological Completion Theorem}{cosmo-completion}
  The physical universe may be interpreted as a
  regenerative distinction ecology evolving under capacity
  transport, constraint accumulation, repair, and
  admissibility preservation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Chapters~16--18 established $\Phi$ as distinction
  capacity, $\mathbf{v}$ as capacity transport, $S$ as
  constraint accumulation, and expyrotic renewal as repair
  (\cref{thm:universe-repair}). Chapters~11--15 established
  regeneration, reachability, and admissibility as abstract
  structures. Combining these — physical fields realizing
  abstract structures — yields a regenerative distinction
  ecology operating at cosmological scale.
\end{proof}

This gives Part~VI a complete logical arc:
\[
  \text{RSVP} \to \text{Gravity} \to \text{Expyrosis}
  \to \text{Cosmological Completion},
\]
and, more importantly, closes the loop back to
Chapters~7--15. The universe is no longer merely a
collection of fields evolving in spacetime. It becomes the
largest instance of the same hierarchy already developed
throughout the book:
\[
  \text{Distinction} \to \text{Repair} \to \text{Regeneration}
  \to \text{Admissibility}.
\]
At this point the physical realization section is
structurally complete, and the cognitive realization
section of Part~VII can begin.


Related Frameworks

\label{sec:ch19-related-frameworks}

\subsection*{Dynamical Systems Theory: Asymptotic Regimes}

\emph{Convergence.} Chapter~\ref{ch:rsvp} engaged dynamical
systems theory on the mechanics of convergence; this chapter's
concern is the classification of long-run behaviour itself.
Dynamical systems theory's vocabulary for asymptotic
behaviour --- fixed points, periodic orbits, strange attractors
--- supplies the natural language for evaluating cosmological
end states \parencite{strogatz2015,arnold1989}. The No Terminal
Equilibrium Theorem and Asymptotic Saturation Theorem
(\cref{thm:no-terminal-equilibrium,thm:asymptotic-saturation})
are framed in exactly this vocabulary: does the cosmological
trajectory converge to a fixed point, or does it not?

\emph{Divergence.} Classical dynamical systems theory treats
fixed points neutrally --- a stable fixed point is one possible
long-run behaviour among others, with no inherent preference for
it over a limit cycle or a strange attractor beyond questions of
stability. This chapter's No Terminal Equilibrium Theorem argues
that a true fixed point would constitute a cosmological failure
mode specifically: a state of zero distinction-production
capacity, the dynamical analogue of heat death, and proves the
system described here cannot reach one. The Asymptotic Saturation
Theorem then characterises the actual long-run behaviour as
saturation --- bounded but non-vanishing ongoing activity ---
a third regime that the standard fixed-point/periodic/chaotic
taxonomy does not single out as a named category in its own
right.

\emph{Open question.} Whether saturation in the sense proved here
is equivalent to convergence toward some already-studied
attracting structure --- a slow manifold, for instance, or an
attracting set of measure zero but nonempty interior in a
suitable quotient space --- or constitutes a genuinely distinct
asymptotic regime not yet named in the existing taxonomy, is left
open.

\section*{Chapter Summary}
\begin{chapsummary}
  \item Cosmological distinction capacity
        $\mathcal{C} + \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{hidden}}$ is
        conserved (\cref{thm:cosmo-capacity}).
  \item The distinction horizon marks loss of
        reachability, not loss of existence
        (\cref{thm:dist-horizon-thm}).

        successive cosmological cycles
        (\cref{thm:cycle-memory}).
  \item Expyrotic renewal is formally a repair operator
        (\cref{thm:universe-repair}).
  \item Permanent heat death is impossible while renewal
        remains possible (\cref{thm:no-terminal-equilibrium}).
  \item Generatively admissible cosmological trajectories
        maximize asymptotic distinction capacity
        (\cref{thm:cosmo-optimality}).
  \item The universe is a regenerative distinction ecology
        at cosmological scale
        (\cref{thm:cosmo-completion}), completing Part~VI.
  \item Local distinction production saturates,
        $dV_D/dt\to0$, while global admissible volume never
        falls to permanent zero,
        $\limsup\Vol(\mathcal{A}_t)>0$
        (\cref{thm:asymptotic-saturation}): saturation is not
        exhaustion.
\end{chapsummary}

Meaning as Distinction Structure


The traditional philosophical question asks what meanings
are: abstract objects, mental representations, social
norms, use patterns, or functional roles.
The distinction-theoretic framework dissolves this
dispute by reframing it.
Meanings are not things. Meanings are geometric
structures over distinction spaces.
